Start with the surfaces, not the size of the building.

A small storefront, a residential driveway and a strata’s shared walkways need different work. Measure the surfaces the crew will actually clear: pedestrian paths, steps, driveway pavement and agreed parking areas. The building’s interior square footage is not the clearing area.

Identify front and rear access separately. Mark ramps, slopes, narrow gates, parked vehicles and places where snow can safely be stored. A large open lot may be easier to service per square metre than a smaller property with stairs and several disconnected paths. An aerial outline helps start a conversation; it cannot confirm every obstacle or access condition.

The basic snow removal pricing formula.

Service cost = labour + equipment + materials + travel + allocated overhead.

Price with a markup = service cost × (1 + markup).

Labour starts with the cost per worker per hour, multiplied by the number of workers and their working time. Include the relevant employment costs when establishing that hourly cost. Equipment may include a truck, plow, spreader or hand tools, together with their operating and maintenance allowance. Materials include the agreed treatment product and quantity.

Travel and route positioning also matter. Overhead covers the share of costs such as administration, insurance and dispatch assigned to the work. Count each cost once: if an equipment allowance already includes fuel, do not add the same fuel again. A customer billing rate may already include overhead and profit, so it should not be treated as a raw wage cost.

Markup and margin are different.

Here is an arithmetic example, not a PlowWow rate or a disclosure of our operating costs. Suppose the defined cost of a visit is $200. Adding a 25% markup produces a $250 selling price. The $50 difference is 20% of that selling price.

To target a 25% margin on that same cost base, the calculation is $200 ÷ (1 − 0.25), giving approximately $266.67 before any applicable tax. The margin percentage must be below 100%. Whether a calculated margin is gross or net depends on which costs were included; a contribution above direct costs is not automatically net profit.

Normal snowfall is the starting assumption.

PlowWow’s starting estimates assume normal-condition snowfall events and standard clearing surfaces. Deep accumulation, heavy wet snow, compacted snow, repeated storm visits, hauling and unusually difficult access can change the work required. A starting price should never imply that every storm and every condition are included.

A useful written proposal defines the service trigger, clearing boundaries, expected response arrangements, snow storage, treatment materials and how additional work is approved. Ask how ongoing snowfall is handled and whether a return visit is a separate charge. Those details make two proposals easier to compare than a headline price alone.

Ice treatment deserves its own line.

Snow clearing and ice treatment are related services, but they are not identical. A site may need treatment on a day with no new snowfall. Shaded entrances, drainage, freezing rain and changing temperatures can all affect the service plan.

Keep combined snow-and-treatment visits separate from additional treatment-only visits when building a winter budget. Do not count the same application twice. Product selection and application depend on the site and conditions; this article does not set a universal material quantity or brine application rate.

Per visit, a package or a seasonal agreement?

A per-visit arrangement prices a defined service when required, subject to the agreed availability and terms. A visit package groups an agreed number of services. A seasonal agreement sets obligations over specified dates and may include different limits, exclusions or readiness commitments.

A planning calculation can multiply a visit allowance by an assumed number of visits, but that is not automatically a fixed seasonal offer. Check whether treatment-only visits, extraordinary storms, dedicated equipment and any service caps are included. Payment timing is a separate term; dividing a budget into monthly amounts does not establish an approved financing plan.

Turn the formula into your property estimate.

For residential properties, identify the driveway, sidewalk frontage, entrance and any agreed rear route. For commercial and strata sites, list shared paths, parking areas, loading access and ramps separately. Use the same scope when comparing providers.

THE RIGHT EQUIPMENT. A CLEARER QUOTE.

Snow plow & equipment costs.

Truck and salter, skid steer or compact quad setup: compare the complete working combination, including the operator and any travel or delivery. Equipment is selected after checking your access, clearing area and snow-storage plan.

Truck + plow + salter

For vehicle-accessible driveways, parking areas and connecting routes. The quote identifies the truck, blade, spreader and operator as a working combination. Confirm whether fuel, treatment material and the first load are included.

Hourly rate and minimum: request a current quote. Salt application and material quantity must be identified; a fitted spreader does not mean unlimited salt is included.

Skid steer / Bobcat-type equipment

For tight manoeuvring, agreed snow stacking and relocation within the site. Attachment, surface conditions, clearance and storage space affect selection. Transporting the machine to the property can be a separate operation.

Hourly rate, minimum and delivery: request a current quote. Confirm operator, attachment, fuel, loading, unloading and pickup before comparing totals.

COMPACT ACCESS

Quad + plow + salter

A compact combination may suit an accessible path network where width, turning room, gradients and surfaces allow. It does not replace hand work on steps, and suitability must be reviewed.

Combination rate and availability: request a current quote. Confirm the operator, plow and spreader, treatment material, trailer transport and any separate hand crew. We quote the actual configuration rather than assuming every quad has both attachments.

Travel time, delivery and pickup.

A local visit and a machine delivered by trailer have different transport costs. Your proposal should identify the departure point, estimated return travel time, travel billing rate, loading and unloading time, delivery and collection charges. Any tolls, disposal or off-site hauling should be stated separately where applicable. There is no universal travel time for Metro Vancouver; route, dispatch location, access and storm conditions matter.

A transparent equipment quote

Agreed billable operating hours × the agreed machine-and-operator rate
+ agreed travel hours × the agreed travel rate
+ agreed delivery / pickup
+ materials and other authorized work not already included.

Apply any minimum hours according to the written terms. A minimum is not automatically an additional fee on top of the same hours. Do not add an hourly equipment charge to a per-visit or seasonal price for work already covered, or bill travel again when it is already included in delivery.

Is the hourly operating cost the same as the customer rate?

No. Internal operating cost covers the defined labour, fuel, maintenance, wear, ownership or rental and allocated overhead. The customer billing rate also needs to support the business and its agreed service obligations. Our public calculator estimates customer service budgets; it does not claim to calculate your contractor’s internal fleet costs.

Can I use a machine’s hourly rate to price a whole snowfall?

Only if the agreed hours and complete scope are known. Hand clearing, treatment-only returns, materials, transport and off-site hauling may have different terms. A per-visit or seasonal proposal should say which equipment work is included and which additional work requires authorization.
