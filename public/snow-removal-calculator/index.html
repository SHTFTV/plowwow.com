What does the snow removal calculator actually tell me?

The PlowWow calculator gives you a rough starting estimate for combined residential snow clearing at a Google-located address. It is an introduction to a reviewed quote, not a measurement survey or a completed winter agreement. You enter a street number, street and city, and see the combined residential starting estimate. There are no metres or square-foot fields to complete before you can move forward.

That simplicity is deliberate. Many property owners know the entrance they want kept clear but do not have a scaled site drawing. Start with the address. Driveway, adjacent sidewalk, walkway and front steps are included together. If the property has shared roads, multiple buildings, a large parking area or a parkade ramp, the standard residential estimate does not price that larger scope. PlowWow can then review the actual access and scope rather than applying a small-property example to a different job.

Seasonal route pricing: what the starting rate covers

The starting rates shown here are for properties joining a planned PlowWow winter route. They describe the service price used in building seasonal coverage, rather than a promise to dispatch a crew for an isolated request at the same rate. A one-off clear, urgent callout or property outside an available route needs its own quote. Read the route condition with the price before sending your details.

A planned route allows the team to consider nearby properties, equipment movement and the service obligations already agreed for that winter. Your final proposal still needs to identify what is included, which conditions trigger work and how exceptional conditions are handled. Do not multiply a displayed visit price by a guessed number of snowfalls and treat the result as a seasonal contract. Ask PlowWow to confirm the agreement that suits your property.

Google finds the address; PlowWow reviews the work

When you request an estimate, the calculator sends your address search to Google Maps. The returned address and satellite map help you check that the correct property has been found. If the result points to the wrong street or municipality, correct the entry before continuing. A complete address is more useful than a development name alone, especially where a complex has more than one entrance.

Google’s address lookup does not identify every surface that needs snow clearing. The tool does not automatically measure your driveway, count steps, assess a ramp or decide where snow can be stored. Its starting price uses a standard residential clearing scope. For the reviewed quote, describe the actual areas you want included. Photographs or a simple marked site plan can help later, but you do not need to measure the property to start this calculator.

House driveways, walkways and storefront entrances

The combined residential starting estimate includes a driveway, adjacent sidewalk, walkway, front steps and entrance. Commercial lots and shared properties require their own confirmed scope and price.

Two properties with the same street address format can require very different work. A short open driveway is different from a long curved access with little room to place snow. A front path is different from several stairs, gates and narrow side passages. The calculator keeps the first step short, while the quote review establishes the service boundaries. Tell PlowWow about steep approaches, fragile finishes, gates, limited clearance and anything a crew should know before arriving.

Strata snow removal starts with shared service boundaries

For strata councils and property managers, the important question is which shared routes the winter plan covers. List the main pedestrian entrances, common paths, internal vehicle access, visitor parking and any parkade approach that needs review. Identify private areas separately. A unit count alone does not tell a contractor the length, layout or difficulty of the shared routes.

The displayed residential starting estimate does not cover an entire strata complex; larger properties need a tailored review. Bring the current site plan if you have one, along with the property contact and priorities. Confirm who can approve additional work and who should receive service questions. A clear route plan helps residents, managers and contractors understand the same boundaries before the season begins.

Commercial access, loading areas and opening times

A commercial snow plan should reflect how people use the property. Identify the entrances customers and staff need, the vehicle circulation that supports the business and any loading or receiving area with restricted access. Tell PlowWow about opening times, delivery windows and places where parked vehicles may obstruct clearing. These details help explain why frontage service and full-lot service are separate discussions.

The online starting estimate is not a commitment to clear every surface at a commercial address. Use the quote request to describe the work you actually need and any priority sequence. If only the front entrance is in scope, say so. If a lot, lane or shared access must also be serviced, include it in the review. Confirm the agreed scope in the written proposal rather than relying on a building name or a photograph alone.

Snow, ice and the visit plan

Snow clearing and ice treatment should be described together in a winter proposal, while making the individual obligations clear. Ask which areas are cleared, which areas are treated, what triggers attendance and how changing conditions are handled. A forecast, a snowfall count and a completed service visit are different pieces of information. The calculator does not forecast the number of visits your winter will require.

Let PlowWow know about recurring shaded areas, drainage routes or access points that require attention during a refreeze. These observations help explain the property; they do not establish a treatment specification by themselves. For current forecasts and alerts, use official weather information as part of your planning. Your agreed service terms determine the contractor’s obligations, rather than an informal assumption that a mild forecast means no winter preparation is needed.

Equipment, snow storage and unusual access

Equipment should fit the actual work and the available access. A plow truck, hand-clearing crew, snow blower and skid steer solve different parts of a winter job. Narrow gates, stairs, parked vehicles and turning space can affect how the property is serviced. Describe those constraints in the quote request instead of selecting a service by the largest machine you have seen.

Also identify where cleared snow may be placed and which routes must remain unobstructed. Moving snow within an agreed storage area is different from loading and hauling it away. A ramp, limited storage, heavy accumulation or compacted material may require a separate allowance or revised plan. PlowWow reviews those details before confirming a price. The starting rate is useful only when the scope and conditions behind it are understood.
