# City Plows Don’t Cover Your Whole Property: Plan Your Richmond Strata Snow Service

![PlowWow Richmond winter planning diagram: sidewalk frontage, private access and condition checks](/blog-images/richmond-strata-private-snow-service-plan.jpg)

Plan the sidewalk, private access and return checks as separate parts of your Richmond winter service. Original planning diagram: PlowWow.

## What happens between the road and your front door?

A cleared road is only one part of a resident’s journey. The driveway entrance, pedestrian path, accessible parking and building doors still need a coordinated plan. If you are arranging snow service for a Richmond condo or townhouse complex, start by listing those areas. A quote is more useful when the provider can see the actual layout, rather than receiving a request to clear everything without knowing what everything includes.

## Understand the City’s service boundary

Richmond gives priority to major roads and bus routes; smaller subdivision roads are outside its designated clearing routes. Check the City’s published route information for the roads around your building. Then mark where your property service starts. Avoid planning private access around an assumed City arrival time. The contractor should know how the driveway connects to the road and who reports a blocked entrance after nearby road clearing.

## Put the sidewalk in the written scope

Adjacent sidewalks must be cleared of snow and ice by 10 a.m. each day, including Sundays. Identify the frontage and confirm who handles it. Discuss the working schedule with the provider rather than assuming a parking-lot plowing visit includes hand work on the sidewalk. The service brief should show stairs, ramps and entrance paths separately, with the agreed treatment and reporting arrangements for each area.

## Ask what starts a visit

Discuss the conditions that trigger dispatch, how the site is monitored and how follow-up is requested. Snow clearing and ice treatment are related tasks, but they may have different triggers and equipment. Ask what happens during frost without substantial snowfall, or when rain and meltwater refreeze. Get the answer into the service agreement. A seasonal contract should make those decisions easier to understand before the first urgent call.

## Make room for equipment and residents

Identify gates, turning areas, overhead limits, parked vehicles, bins and delivery access. Tell the provider which entrances must remain available and where pedestrian work needs to be coordinated with equipment movement. Agree how residents will be notified if vehicles need moving. Include the main and backup operational contact, and share access credentials directly rather than placing them on a publicly circulated notice or an open website.

## Choose storage before snow piles up

Review proposed snow-storage areas with the contractor. Consider walking routes, accessible stalls, sightlines, drains and the direction meltwater will travel. Keep the map specific enough that a replacement crew can use it. If storage becomes limited, discuss how relocation or hauling would be assessed and authorised. Extra equipment or hauling is a separate scope decision; confirm it before asking residents to expect a particular result.

## Protect sensitive surfaces

Point out recent concrete work, stamped pads, pavers, raised utility covers and existing damage while they are visible. Provide installer instructions for any finish that limits scraping or deicer use. Ask the provider to review equipment and treatment methods for those areas. Keep dated photographs in the property file and the approved restrictions in the dispatch notes. Product selection should match the surface and conditions, rather than the colour of a bag.
