# TruckMaxx EXT: A 19-Foot Plow Meets Greater Vancouver Parking Lots

**By the PlowWow team · November 17, 2026 · Equipment enthusiasm**

Some equipment makes you stop scrolling. Metal Pless’s TruckMaxx EXT is one of those machines. A wide blade that can change its working configuration is an impressive piece of equipment, especially when you picture a long, open parking-lot lane and a sensible place to put the snow.

We do not own this 19-foot unit, and this is not a PlowWow test report. We do have a 16-foot parking-lot pusher that we call our **parking-lot crusher**. That is why equipment built to work broad commercial pavement gets our attention. Bigger can be useful. The interesting question is where the extra width fits the whole job.

## What Metal Pless currently lists

The [TruckMaxx EXT product page](https://www.metal-pless.com/products/truckmaxx-ext) lists model **TRK EXT 1042-19**, a **10-foot box dimension**, **19-foot extended blade dimension**, **42-inch height** and approximately **4,340 pounds**. It identifies trucks and wheel loaders in a **30,000–50,000-pound application range** and currently marks availability as **2027**. Confirm the actual carrier, mounting, hydraulics and configuration with the manufacturer or dealer.

This is not a fresh first unveiling. The company’s [October 18, 2025 podcast episode notes](https://podcasts.apple.com/us/podcast/1-whats-new-at-metal-pless/id1846961908?i=1000732377571) already linked TruckMaxx EXT demonstrations ahead of Equip Expo 2025. Earlier demonstrations and the current availability listing describe different things.

## Why that adjustable width gets us excited

For a Greater Vancouver retail plaza or commercial site, the attractive part is the ability to match the equipment’s footprint to different portions of the property. An open lane offers a different opportunity from a storefront corner. A long push toward an agreed storage area is a different task from detail work beside parked cars.

Our enthusiasm is for that flexibility, not a made-up percentage. On a suitable uninterrupted lane, a broader usable blade can cover more pavement across the direction of travel. That geometric opportunity does not establish a finish time, salt saving or number of trucks replaced. The material being moved and everything at either end of the pass still count.

## Nineteen feet is not a constant clearing swath

A fully extended blade dimension is not automatically the width of the strip cleared while the blade is angled. Turning a straight blade away from square to the direction of travel reduces its transverse projection; wing position, overlap and the actual operating configuration matter too. Do not substitute an illustration or a headline dimension for the manufacturer’s working-width information.

Nor does a smaller box dimension prove that the complete carrier-and-plow combination can travel through any opening of that size. Turning envelope, mounting and transport requirements need their own checks. We would want the exact working and transport dimensions before planning a route around the equipment.

## Islands and parked cars decide where the width is useful

Picture a **hypothetical** commercial lot: a broad central aisle, islands along one row, cars staying overnight and a delivery entrance at the far end. The open aisle might suit a broad pass. The island noses and the occupied parking row can require a different configuration, another direction of approach or separate equipment.

Changing blade width does not remove the truck’s turning circle or create clearance beside a parked car. Operators still need visibility, safe separation and a plan for obstacles. A wide machine should earn its place through the clear pavement it can use, rather than by trying to carry its maximum footprint into every corner.

## The snow still needs a destination

A parking-lot clearing plan should identify storage locations before the first push. Keep entrances, pedestrian connections, accessible parking access aisles, loading areas and sightlines in that discussion. A long productive lane is much less useful if it finishes at a pile location that blocks the next task.

For properties in Vancouver, Burnaby, Richmond, Surrey and the wider Lower Mainland, the site assessment needs to account for the actual layout and agreed priorities. Snow depth and weight, drainage, remaining vehicles and available storage all affect the work. This is site-planning commentary, not a claim that the demonstrated machine has been tested on those properties.
