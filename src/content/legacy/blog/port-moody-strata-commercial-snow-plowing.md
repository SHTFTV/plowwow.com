## 2. The Realities of the Port Moody Microclimate: Shade Traps & Steep Runs

Port Moody’s geography creates a specialized microclimate that differs significantly from neighboring Coquitlam or Burnaby. Bordered by steep hillsides locally known as "The North Shore of the Tri-Cities" and the damp influence of the Inlet, the city experiences what we call "Coastal Valley Shade Traps."

Areas like Heritage Mountain and Pleasantside often sit in the shadow of the mountains during the short winter days. This lack of solar gain means that even if the ambient temperature rises to 3°C, road surfaces in shaded strata complexes remain below freezing. This leads to persistent "black ice" long after the sun has come out elsewhere.

## 3. Navigating the Legal Landscape: BC Occupiers Liability Act & Bylaws

For Port Moody property owners, the legal stakes of snow removal are dictated by the **BC Occupiers Liability Act**. Under this legislation, a strata corporation has a "duty of care" to ensure that anyone entering the premises is reasonably safe. In the eyes of the court, "I couldn't find a plow" is rarely an acceptable defense.

Furthermore, Port Moody municipal bylaws require that snow and ice be cleared from sidewalks adjacent to commercial and multi-family properties within 24 hours (or sooner depending on specific zoning). Failure to comply results in fines, but more importantly, it opens the door to slip-and-fall litigation that can increase insurance premiums for years.

## 6. Overcoming Common Operational Pitfalls: Parkades & Catch Basins

Two major issues plague Port Moody stratas every winter: frozen parkade ramps and blocked catch basins.

**Underground Parkade Ramps:** In many Port Moody condos, the parkade ramp is exposed to the elements. If a layer of ice forms on the incline, the complex becomes a prison—residents cannot get out for work, and emergency vehicles cannot get in. **PlowWow** prioritizes ramp de-icing with high-concentration liquids that penetrate ice faster than traditional rock salt.

**Catch Basin Management:** During the inevitable West Coast "melt-off," snow piles block the catch basins. When the rain starts, the strata turns into a lake. This leads to property damage and ice re-freezing.
