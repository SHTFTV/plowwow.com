# Winter Service Planning for Mixed-Use Properties

A building with homes above shops can have several people making decisions about the same outdoor space. Residents need access to the lobby and parking, retailers have opening times, and deliveries may share a narrow service lane. A winter service plan should make those relationships explicit before anyone is asked to price or schedule the work.

![PlowWow mascot beside a cleared strata walkway in a winter setting](/blog-images/mixed-use-property-winter-service-coordination.jpg "PlowWow winter property planning")

## Identify who manages each area

Create a drawing that distinguishes the residential entrance, commercial storefronts, parkade access, common walkways and service areas. Confirm responsibilities with the property’s existing management arrangements. Where two scopes meet, agree exactly where one provider’s work ends and the other begins. Do not leave a shared stair or walkway dependent on an assumption that somebody else has included it.

## Build a combined access schedule

Collect the time-sensitive needs of the residential and commercial parts of the property. Include staff arrivals, waste collection and regular deliveries. Explain gated access and height or turning restrictions directly to the contractor. Ask for a proposed work sequence that reflects the site, and confirm which requests can be accommodated before communicating the plan to occupants.

## Agree how changes will be approved

Choose a lead contact who can reconcile competing requests and a backup who can be reached when that person is unavailable. Establish who can authorize additional work and how costs will be allocated under the property’s arrangements. If a tenant requests a change, have it reviewed against the existing scope before directing crews to perform it.

## Communicate a practical occupant plan

Tell residents and commercial occupants where to report concerns, which areas need to remain accessible and whether any vehicle movement is required. Keep public notices concise and share sensitive access details separately. After the first service visit, collect specific feedback about access or unclear boundaries and discuss any necessary revision with the provider.
