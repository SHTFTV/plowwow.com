# Where does the second storm’s snow go?

![PlowWow winter photograph of the parking area and frontage at 5549 Kingsway in Burnaby](/blog-images/kingsway-5549-building-snow-plowwow.jpg)

*PlowWow’s own winter photo, 5549 Kingsway, Burnaby. The plaza example below is illustrative.*

Picture a small retail plaza the morning after a snowfall. The entrance is clear and customers can park. A pile fills three stalls at the far end. Then the grocery tenant points out that the empty space beside it is where Thursday’s delivery truck swings wide. With another storm coming, where does the next push go?

## Walk the delivery turn before choosing the pile

Meet the property manager and the person who receives deliveries while the pavement markings are visible. Follow the truck’s approach, turn and exit. Next, walk from the accessible stalls to the entrance. Mark those paths on a copy of the lot plan along with doors, crossings, drains and areas the site has designated to remain clear. A corner that looks empty on an aerial photo may be busy for ten minutes every morning.

Circle the proposed first pile. Then draw its footprint again, larger. If the second outline reaches the truck’s turn or the pedestrian route, agree on another location or a haul-out decision before the storm. Leave the marked plan with the site contact and crew lead.

## Agree on the call that starts haul-out

Choose an observable trigger. For the example plaza, it might be the point where further stacking would occupy the marked delivery turn. Record who can approve hauling, who covers after hours and how the contractor will price the work. Confirm where hauled snow can be accepted before dispatching trucks. A manager can then make a decision from a photo and a marked plan rather than guessing what “almost full” means.

## Use the building QR code when the pile reaches that point

PlowWow’s Snow Intelligence signs have a QR code specific to each building. Scan it, confirm the address on the property page, then tap call or text. The text option opens your messaging app with the building address in the draft. Add the location and what has changed before sending. For example: “East loading entrance, beside stall 12. The pile now reaches the painted turn. Tomorrow’s grocery delivery is at 7 a.m.” A photo from the same viewpoint used on the plan helps the team compare conditions.
