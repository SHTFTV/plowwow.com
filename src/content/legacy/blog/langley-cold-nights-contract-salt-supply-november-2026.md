# Langley’s colder nights: check the contract and salt supply

![PlowWow mascot Wow reviewing a clipboard beside covered bagged treatment material and a stationary broadcast spreader](/blog-images/langley-cold-nights-contract-salt-supply-november-2026.jpg "PlowWow winter-service contract and material-supply planning")

**By the PlowWow team · November 15, 2026 · Winter Planning**

A colder night matters before the first snow-clearing visit. For a Langley homeowner, it is a reason to decide how the front-door route will be covered. For a strata or commercial manager, it is a reason to check who can approve service, what the contract includes and whether the material-supply plan is actually confirmed.

Do that work while there is time to resolve a question. A changing forecast is useful planning information; it does not reserve a crew or tell you the condition of every surface.

## What the November 15 outlook actually says

A [Weather Network chart](https://www.theweathernetwork.com/en/city/ca/british-columbia/langley/14-days) captured at **12:44 p.m. Pacific on November 15, 2026** showed daytime highs around **8–10°C for November 16–27**, with lows near **1°C around November 18–19**. Those are figures from that dated outlook, not an observation at a particular property.

The [official Environment and Climate Change Canada Langley forecast](https://weather.gc.ca/en/location/index.html?coords=49.104%2C-122.659), issued at **11 a.m. Pacific on November 15**, differed: it listed a Monday high of **11°C** and a **+1°C low for Thursday night, November 19**. Different providers and forecast periods can give different numbers. Check the current forecast and alerts again before making an operating decision.

Neither outlook is a promise of snow. The useful next step is to close gaps in the winter plan.

## A temperature icon is not a surface assessment

ECCC explains that rain or drizzle can freeze when it meets a surface below zero. That is a description of a hazard, not a claim that freezing rain is forecast for Langley this week. A near-freezing air forecast alone does not establish whether your entrance, parking area or walkway needs treatment. [Read ECCC’s winter-hazard guidance](https://www.canada.ca/en/environment-climate-change/services/seasonal-weather-hazards/be-prepared-for-winter.html).

Agree who checks conditions, who receives a concern and how the agreed service is requested or triggered. Describe the location and what was observed instead of treating every cool night as an automatic salting instruction.

## Make the property plan specific enough to use

Start with the route people need: the common entrance, the path between parking and the door, shared walkway stairs, and any separately agreed areas. For a business, add opening hours and delivery access. For a homeowner, identify whether the quote is for a walkway, driveway or both.

A marked photograph helps distinguish an included shared route from an individual-unit path. Show gates, parked-vehicle constraints and proposed on-site snow storage. Ask about ramps, extra areas or hauling explicitly; do not assume that they are included because they are close to the main route.

If the property changes after the quote, send the change for review. A new gate or added clearing area can change the work.

## Choose a booking arrangement, then confirm its limits

PlowWow’s [residential booking options](/residential-snow-removal) distinguish a seasonal plan, a five-visit booking and on-call service. They are different arrangements:

- **Seasonal:** repeat visits for the work and service dates in the written agreement. Read the return-window arrangements and extreme-storm charges as part of that agreement.
- **Five-visit booking:** a separate residential route option for five snow-clearing visits. Ask how visits are recorded and what happens when additional work is needed.
- **On-call:** check route availability when requesting service. Do not treat an enquiry or an unconfirmed request as an accepted booking.

Unlimited seasonal visits do not mean every task, every surface or an immediate return on demand. The [Unlimited Clarification Policy](/unlimited-clarification-policy) explains the existing scope, normal-condition route target and extreme-event terms. Your signed agreement sets the property-specific arrangement.

For strata and commercial properties, ask for the reviewed proposal that fits the site. A residential starting rate is not a citywide price for a parking lot.

## Confirm salt supply before you need service

“Salt is covered” leaves several questions unanswered. Clarify who supplies the agreed treatment material, how its cost is handled and who checks replenishment. For per-visit residential bookings, PlowWow’s current page separates a salt-only visit charge from the salt or ice-melt product used; ask how that applies to the accepted scope.

If a property also keeps material for its own authorized staff, use a short procurement record:

- **Product:** the actual product name and packaging, with the manufacturer’s handling and storage information available.
- **Supply:** the supplier, confirmed order status and any current collection or delivery arrangement.
- **Responsibility:** who checks the stock, who can approve another order and who contacts the contractor about the contracted areas.
- **Handover:** where the record is kept and who receives an update if material or access is unavailable.

Confirm availability and lead time with the supplier, record the order status and follow the product’s handling and storage guidance. Treatment decisions still depend on actual property conditions and the agreed service scope.

## Make the first morning’s handover clear

[WorkSafeBC identifies water, ice, obstructed walkways and poor lighting as slip or trip concerns](https://www.worksafebc.com/en/health-safety/hazards-exposures/slips-trips-falls). Give residents or staff a clear way to report the location of a concern. Keep a current property contact and an alternate contact in the agreed service record.

Decide how a report becomes an assessed service request and how completed work is communicated. The aim is a usable handover between the person on site and the service provider, rather than several people assuming someone else has called.
