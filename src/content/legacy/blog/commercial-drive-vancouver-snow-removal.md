## Why Commercial Drive Snow Removal Is a Retail Problem, Not a Residential One

The Drive is 22 uninterrupted retail block-faces from Venables to East 13th, wrapped around the Grandview-Woodland residential neighborhood on both sides. That geography changes what "snow removal" actually means here:

- **Every storefront is its own liability.** The City of Vancouver Street and Traffic By-law requires each occupier to clear the sidewalk adjacent to their property by 10 a.m. the day after a snowfall. On The Drive, that's 200+ individual shopfronts, each of which is one slip-and-fall away from an insurance claim.
- **Independent operators have no back-of-house snow crew.** A single-location cafe on Commercial and 5th doesn't have a facilities team. If PlowWow doesn't clear the sidewalk, no one does.
- **6:30 a.m. is the deadline.** JJ Bean, Prado, Turks, Continental — first pour is between 6:15 and 6:45. The sidewalk needs to be salted before then.
- **Heritage brick and concrete inserts.** Sections of The Drive have decorative brick banding, planter boxes and bike-corral outstands that a suburban plow blade will destroy. Rubber-edge equipment is the only correct tool.
- **Grandview-Woodland strata density.** North and south of The Drive, the Grandview-Woodland grid packs low-rise strata, walk-up rentals, and character homes onto Victoria, Salsbury, Semlin, Lakewood and Woodland. Every one of those buildings has a walkway, a stair set and a curb ramp that needs clearing.
