## The Realities of the Pitt Meadows Microclimate

Pitt Meadows does not behave like the elevated communities to its east. Instead of mountain runoff, the dominant threat here is **radiation fog and frost pooling**. On clear, calm nights following a snow event, cold, dense air sinks off the surrounding farmland and settles over Bonson, South Bonson, and the low-lying commercial corridor near the airport, forming a shallow frost layer that can linger long after surrounding municipalities have thawed.

The second factor is the **Pitt River channel effect**. Cold air funnels down the Pitt River valley from Pitt Lake, accelerating wind chill across open parking lots and dike-adjacent industrial yards near Airport Way. Combined with the flat terrain — there is nowhere for cold air to drain away — this creates prolonged icing windows on commercial lots that would clear naturally in a hillier municipality.

## Navigating the Legal Landscape: Liability and Bylaws

Under the **BC Occupiers Liability Act**, every property owner or manager in British Columbia must take "reasonable care" to protect people on the premises. Courts routinely treat the absence of a documented snow removal plan — or a professional service agreement with a provider like [plowwow.com](https://plowwow.com) — as evidence that duty of care was not met.

Pitt Meadows enforces this locally through its **Boulevard Maintenance Bylaw No. 2377 (2008)**, which requires owners and occupiers to clear snow and ice from the sidewalk adjacent to their property by **10:00 AM** the day following a snowfall. Non-compliance can result in fines of **up to $100** per violation, and — more significantly — becomes a documented failure point if a slip-and-fall claim follows.

## Overcoming Common Operational Pitfalls

Managing a Pitt Meadows property involves more than pushing snow off pavement — the floodplain setting introduces failure points that hillier municipalities rarely see.

### Dike-Top Wind Exposure
Properties backing onto the Pitt River or Fraser River dikes see snow drift laterally across parking lots and loading areas, re-covering pavement that was already cleared. Without a scheduled re-pass, "clean" lots can look untouched within an hour of a plow visit.

### Drainage Saturation & Refreeze
Because Pitt Meadows sits below river level and relies on pump-assisted drainage, meltwater has nowhere to go during a hard freeze. Water pools in low commercial lots and refreezes overnight into sheet ice that is easy to miss on a routine walk-through.
