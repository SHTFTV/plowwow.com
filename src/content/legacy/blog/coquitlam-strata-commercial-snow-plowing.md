## The Realities of the Coquitlam Microclimate: Defeating Burke Mountain Snow Line Traps

Coquitlam is unique in the Lower Mainland for its dramatic elevation changes. While Maillardville or the City Centre might see a cold drizzle, Burke Mountain and Westwood Plateau are often enduring "mountain dumps"—heavy, moisture-laden snow that can accumulate at a rate of several centimeters per hour.

These "Snow Line Traps" occur when warm, moist Pacific air hits the cold air masses descending from the Coquitlam and Pitt Lake mountains. The result is "Heart Attack Snow"—a dense, heavy slush that settles into ice within hours. Standard light-duty trucks often fail in these conditions, bogged down by the sheer weight of the precipitation.

## Navigating the Legal Landscape: BC Occupiers Liability Act & Coquitlam Bylaws

In British Columbia, the law is clear: property owners and occupiers (including Strata Corporations) have a "Duty of Care" to ensure that anyone entering the premises is reasonably safe. Under the *BC Occupiers Liability Act*, ignorance of the weather is not a valid defense. If a resident or visitor slips on your strata lot, the legal team will scrutinize your snow removal logbooks.

Furthermore, the **City of Coquitlam Highway and Traffic Bylaw** requires that all owners or occupiers of commercial or industrial buildings—as well as multi-family residential developments—clear snow and ice from sidewalks adjacent to their property within a strict timeframe (often by 10:00 am following a snowfall).

## Overcoming Common Operational Pitfalls: Parkades & Catch Basins

Coquitlam's "freeze-thaw" cycle is notorious. During the day, snow melts into slush; at night, it drains into catch basins and onto parkade ramps where it refreezes as sheer black ice. This is the primary cause of vehicle accidents within strata lots.

Underground parkade ramps are specifically dangerous. If the ramp is not heated (or if the heating system fails), it becomes a slide. Our technicians at **plowwow.com** pay special attention to these "friction zones."
