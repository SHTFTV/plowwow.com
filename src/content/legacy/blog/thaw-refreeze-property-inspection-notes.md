## Describe the surface, not just the forecast

A warmer afternoon does not tell you the condition of every entrance later in the evening. A useful winter note records the location, time and visible conditions at the property. It does not label the entire site safe because one area looks clear.

Weather information provides context. The practical follow-up is to check the routes people actually use and communicate observations through the agreed property process. Avoid entering a hazardous area just to obtain a closer photograph.

## Follow the water

Look from an appropriate position at where meltwater collects or crosses a walking route. Note low spots, shaded transitions and locations already known to the property team. Record what is observed without assuming a drainage fault or recommending an unqualified repair.

A recurring issue deserves a separate maintenance conversation. Snow service, drainage maintenance and building repairs may involve different people and different approvals. Keep those responsibilities clear.

## Make observations useful to someone else

Replace vague notes such as slippery out back with a clear route description. Include the entrance name, the relevant side of the building and the approximate extent of the observed condition. Use a private site identifier rather than a resident’s name.

A photograph can provide context, but it may not show transparent ice, slope or changing conditions. Pair it with a factual description and avoid editing the image in a way that obscures the original record.

## Confirm the follow-up process

Agree who reviews observations, when another assessment is considered and how additional service is authorized. Do not assume that reporting a concern automatically creates a new booking or changes the existing agreement.

WorkSafeBC’s icy-surface awareness material is useful background for site teams. It does not establish a universal inspection interval. The appropriate process needs to reflect the property, its use and the agreed service scope.

## Keep the next visit focused

Tell PlowWow what changed since the previous visit and which route needs attention. If access is restricted, explain the restriction before a crew arrives. For recurring meltwater, bring previous notes to the person responsible for the building’s maintenance.
