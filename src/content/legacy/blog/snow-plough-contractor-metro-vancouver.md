## What does a snow plough contractor do

A snow plough contractor clears accumulated snow from agreed areas so people and vehicles can move around a property. Depending on the site, the work may involve a plough, smaller clearing equipment or hand shovelling. Ice treatment and snow hauling are separate parts of the conversation.

The best starting point is a map of where you need access. For a homeowner, that could be the driveway, front steps and path to the side gate. For a business, it could include customer parking, staff access and a loading area.

Think about the complete journey. A cleared parking space has limited value if the path to the entrance remains blocked. A wide vehicle lane does not answer the needs of someone using an accessible entrance.

Before comparing quotes, write down the routes that matter. This turns a broad request for “snow removal” into a clear description of the work you actually need.

## Strata snow removal considers every shared route

PlowWow provides [snow removal for strata properties](https://www.plowwow.com/strata-complexes), including townhome communities and condominium developments. Shared roads, driveways, visitor parking, walkways, stairs and entrances can form part of the property's winter service plan.

## Snow clearing and ice management need separate attention

Ploughing removes accumulated snow. Salting and de-icing address icy surfaces. A property may need attention to both, so the quote should make clear which areas receive each service.

For customers, a helpful approach is to identify the places that deserve a closer look: shaded paths, steps, slopes, entrances and areas where water collects. Discuss these locations when reviewing the scope instead of assuming that every treatment is included everywhere.

Keep communication specific during the season. “The rear entrance looks icy” gives a clearer starting point than “the site needs attention.” Include the location and a photo when practical, and use the contact arrangement agreed for your property.

Conditions can change after a visit. Continue to pay attention to the routes you use and report concerns through the agreed process.

## Where will the cleared snow go

Snow needs a destination. Before the season, identify possible storage areas and the spaces that must stay open. Consider parking, entrances, gates, sightlines and drainage when reviewing the layout.

PlowWow offers [on-site snow relocation and off-site hauling](https://www.plowwow.com/snow-relocation) where required. These services can be discussed when accumulated piles begin to affect usable space or when a property has limited room for snow storage. Confirm the work and price before treating it as included.

A planning exercise is to imagine the site after several visits. Would the original piling area still leave room to turn a vehicle or reach a gate? Raising the question early gives you time to consider options before space becomes tight.

## What should your snow removal agreement cover

A useful agreement makes the service understandable. Before accepting a quote, check these six points:

1. **Areas:** Which driveways, lanes, parking spaces, paths, steps and entrances are included?
2. **Work:** Does the plan cover clearing, ice treatment, or both? How are materials handled?
3. **Triggers:** What conditions start service, and how are return visits arranged?
4. **Access:** Who manages gates, parked vehicles and other obstacles?
5. **Communication:** Who should report a problem, and which contact method should they use?
6. **Pricing:** What is included, and what work or conditions can create extra charges?

For PlowWow seasonal plans, your written agreement defines the service dates, covered areas, triggers, return arrangements and pricing. Unlimited seasonal visits apply within the agreed scope; exceptional-weather charges and additional work have their own terms. Review the [current policy](https://www.plowwow.com/unlimited-clarification-policy) alongside your property-specific quote.

Ask questions while the plan is being prepared. It is easier to resolve an unclear boundary or access requirement before the first service visit.

## A simple walkthrough before the first snowfall

Before winter changes the view, walk the agreed service areas and note anything that could become difficult to see. Photograph existing surface damage, raised edges, decorative features and other obstacles. Share relevant details with the person preparing your plan.

Check that access information is current. A gate code that changed last month or a vehicle regularly parked in a turning space can affect how the site is reached. Tell us about changes rather than assuming they will be obvious on arrival.

Keep your own copy of the agreed layout where the responsible people can find it. For a business or strata property, include anyone who may cover the main contact's absence.
