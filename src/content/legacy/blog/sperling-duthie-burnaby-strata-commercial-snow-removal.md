## Sperling-Duthie's Snow Reality — Elevation, Trees, Runoff

The neighbourhood is not the same weather as Metrotown, and treating it like Metrotown is how contracts fail here. Five specific conditions define the winter risk:

- **Elevation.** Sperling-Duthie ranges roughly 100–200 metres above sea level. That translates to 30–50% more accumulation per event than the Burnaby lowland reference stations, and a snow-line that can sit on Duthie Avenue while Hastings Street stays green.
- **Tree canopy.** Douglas fir and cedar canopy along Duthie, Sperling and the Broadview corridor drops needles and drips overnight — creating localized refreeze zones on driveways and sidewalks that surface-lot providers routinely miss.
- **Mountain drainage.** Runoff from the Burnaby Mountain slope crosses Sperling-Duthie streets and drives, and refreezes overnight in shaded gutters and inlet approaches. This is where black ice actually forms in the neighbourhood.
- **SFU commuter routes.** Sperling Avenue, Duthie Avenue and Broadway serve as arterial paths to Simon Fraser University. Clearing has to be finished before the 6:30 a.m. commuter push.
- **Character home + townhome strata mix.** The neighbourhood combines mid-century single-family homes with 1980s and 1990s townhome complexes on shared driveways. Each has a different scope and a different bylaw exposure.

## Burnaby Bylaw and the Sidewalk Question

City of Burnaby Bylaw 7370 (and successor consolidations) place responsibility for clearing snow and ice from public sidewalks adjacent to a property on the property owner or occupier. The window is defined and enforced. In practice, that means:

- **Every character home in Sperling-Duthie has a sidewalk-clearing duty on every snow event.**
- **Every townhome strata is responsible for the full sidewalk perimeter of the complex** — often several hundred metres of continuous frontage.
- **Small-retail landlords are responsible for storefront and side-street sidewalks** on the perimeter of the property.

A snow contract that treats public sidewalks as an "add-on" is under-scoped for Sperling-Duthie. PlowWow includes bylaw sidewalk clearing in every base scope.

## Strata Governance for Mountain-Adjacent Complexes

BC *Strata Property Act* sections 3 and 72 make the strata corporation responsible for common-property snow-and-ice safety. For a mountain-adjacent complex, that responsibility is heavier than average — a shared driveway with a 6% grade and runoff crossing it is genuinely more dangerous than a flat Metrotown driveway, and case law expects the response to reflect that.

Practical council checklist for a Sperling-Duthie strata:

- **Approve a seasonal contract at AGM, on the record.** The minute enters the eventual defence file.
- **Define the trigger in centimetres** (typically 2 cm) and specify freezing-rain forecast triggers separately.
- **Require documented salt-only dispatches** for refreeze mornings.
- **Require photo documentation** of entrance courts, ramp approaches, mail-kiosk paths.
- **Renew before September 30.** Regional capacity is tighter for mountain-adjacent zones because pre-staging is more equipment-intensive.

## Seasonal vs Per-Event — The Sperling-Duthie Math

For a Sperling-Duthie strata or character-home owner, the case for seasonal is driven by three numbers:

1. **Response time on a mountain gradient.** Per-event customers are dispatched *after* seasonal customers. On a 6% grade with mountain runoff, being second in queue can mean four hours of accumulated black ice by the time the crew arrives.
2. **Refreeze frequency.** Mountain-adjacent zones produce more freeze/thaw cycles per winter than the Burnaby lowland — roughly 25–35 refreeze events even in a mild season. Every one is a slip-claim opportunity.
3. **Budget variance.** Per-event bad winters in a mountain-adjacent complex produce genuinely large invoices, sometimes triggering special levies. Seasonal contracts eliminate that variance.
