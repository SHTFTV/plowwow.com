## Why Pushing Snow Onto Roads Is Illegal and Dangerous

Clearview Township is reminding residents that placing snow or ice onto roads isn't just against the law—it's a serious safety hazard for drivers, pedestrians, and winter maintenance crews. If you're clearing your driveway this season, it's critical to understand the legal and safety implications of improper snow disposal.

## It's Illegal Under the Highway Traffic Act

**Legal Requirement:** Under the Highway Traffic Act, "no person shall deposit snow or ice on the roadway." This means pushing snow from your driveway or property onto the road or road shoulders is illegal and can result in fines.

Township officials emphasize that while winter maintenance crews work tirelessly throughout the season to keep roads safe and passable, these efforts are often undermined when residents push snow from private driveways onto roadways. Understanding and following this law protects everyone in your community.

## The Safety Hazards of Road Snow Placement

Snow placed on roads creates several dangerous conditions for drivers and pedestrians:

*   **Hazardous Ice Patches:** During fluctuating winter temperatures, snow on road surfaces quickly becomes hardened, icy patches that are difficult for plows to remove and treacherous for vehicles to drive over.
*   **Reduced Traction:** These icy buildups reduce tire traction and significantly increase the risk of collisions, especially when drivers encounter unexpected packed snow or ice patches.
*   **Reduced Visibility:** Snowbanks and windrows created by improper snow placement block sightlines at intersections, driveways, and crossings—a critical safety issue for both motorists and pedestrians.
*   **Vehicle Damage:** Hardened snow and ice on roadways can damage vehicles, including tires, suspension components, and undercarriages, particularly at higher speeds or during low-visibility conditions.

## Metro Vancouver Snow Removal Bylaws

Residents across Metro Vancouver are subject to specific snow removal bylaws that enforce proper snow management. Here are the key regulations by municipality:

*   **City of Vancouver:** Snow removal regulations are outlined in [Traffic By-law 2849](https://bylaws.vancouver.ca/consolidated/9964.pdf). Learn more on the [official snow removal page](https://vancouver.ca/streets-transportation/snow-removal-from-city-streets.aspx).
*   **North Vancouver:** Snow must be cleared within 24 hours after snowfall or before accumulation exceeds 10cm. Contact: 604-987-7155
*   **Burnaby:** Snow clearing is required by 10 AM daily. Contact: 604-294-7460 or [engineering@burnaby.ca](mailto:engineering@burnaby.ca)
*   **New Westminster:** Snow removal must be completed by 10 AM the day following snowfall. Contact: 604-519-2010

For a comprehensive guide to snow removal bylaws across British Columbia, visit the [Snow Removal Bylaws in British Columbia](https://sci-bc.ca/winter-rules-snow-removal-bylaws-in-british-columbia/) resource. For more Vancouver-specific information, check out [snowremoval.tv/vancouver](https://snowremoval.tv/vancouver).

## How You Can Help Keep Roads Safe

Clearview Township asks all residents to take responsibility for proper snow disposal. Keep snow from your driveways and sidewalks on your own property—never push it onto roads or shoulders. This simple action helps winter maintenance crews complete snow removal more efficiently and keeps roads safer for everyone in your community.

**Remember:** Responsible snow management is a community effort. When you dispose of snow properly, you're protecting your neighbors, emergency responders, and road maintenance crews.

## Questions About Snow Removal?

For more information about winter snow removal guidelines and proper disposal practices in your area, contact your local township office or visit your municipality's official website.

## What You Should Do Instead

*   **Pile snow on your property:** Use your front lawn, garden beds, or other areas on your own land
*   **Check local regulations:** Some cities allow piling on the curb if it doesn't block the road
*   **Request city assistance:** Many municipalities offer free snow removal for seniors or people with disabilities
*   **Hire a licensed contractor:** Ensure they follow local bylaws and dispose of snow properly
*   **Report violations:** Contact your city if neighbors are illegally dumping snow on roads

### Frequently Asked Questions About Snow Removal

**Q: Why is pushing snow onto the road illegal?**

 A: It creates safety hazards for drivers and pedestrians, blocks storm drains, interferes with city snow removal operations, and increases maintenance costs for municipalities.

**Q: What if I don't clear my sidewalk in time?**

 A: Most cities will send contractors to clear it and add the cost to your property tax bill, plus fines ranging from $100-$1,000+ depending on your city.

**Q: Can I pile snow on the curb?**

 A: It depends on your city. Some allow it as long as it doesn't block the road. Check your local bylaw or call 311.

**Q: What's the fastest I need to clear snow?**

 A: Most Canadian cities require clearing within 12-48 hours of snowfall. Toronto requires 12 hours, while most others require 24 hours.

**Q: Are there exceptions for seniors or people with disabilities?**

 A: Yes. Most cities offer free or low-cost snow removal assistance. Contact your city's social services or call 311.
