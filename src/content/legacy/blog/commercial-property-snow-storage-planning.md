# Snow Storage Planning for Commercial Properties

A commercial snow-removal plan needs a place for the cleared snow to go. If a quote describes only the area to plow, the site can still run into trouble when the proposed storage space turns out to be a delivery bay, a pedestrian route or parking needed by tenants. A short site review helps turn those assumptions into a usable plan.

![PlowWow mascot beside a cleared commercial parking area in a winter setting](/blog-images/commercial-property-snow-storage-planning.jpg "PlowWow winter property planning")

## Mark proposed storage areas on a drawing

Start with a site map showing entrances, parking aisles, loading areas, pedestrian routes and property boundaries. Mark possible storage areas and ask the contractor to assess them. Identify locations that must remain clear, including accessible parking and routes between the street and building entrances. Do not assume that an empty-looking neighbouring area is available for your use.

## Make room for the way the property operates

Explain which spaces fill up during business hours and where delivery vehicles turn. A storage location that works overnight may obstruct the next morning’s activity. Discuss how the proposed plan works when vehicles remain parked during service and who can arrange for them to move. Include photographs of tight corners and any features that become difficult to see under snow.

## Discuss accumulation before it becomes a problem

Ask how the contractor will report that a proposed storage area is nearing its useful capacity. Identify who can authorize additional work and how a revised plan will be agreed. Snow relocation within the property and hauling snow away are different scopes; confirm which options are available, what equipment is needed and how pricing is determined. Include any site-access limitations in that discussion.

## Keep the plan available to everyone involved

Save the agreed drawing with the service scope and operational contact details. Share the relevant instructions with tenants and site staff so temporary storage areas are not unexpectedly occupied. After a service visit, note blocked access or changes to the property and update the plan with the contractor. A dated revision is easier to follow than conflicting instructions passed between several people.
