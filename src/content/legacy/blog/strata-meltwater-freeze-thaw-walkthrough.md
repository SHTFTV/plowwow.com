# Where Does Meltwater Go? A Freeze–Thaw Walkthrough for Strata Properties

![A wet parkade ramp and parallel pedestrian walkway bordered by snow](/blog-images/burnaby-hills-parkade-snow-ice-plan.jpg)

*PlowWow illustration of a winter parkade entrance and adjacent walkway.*

**By the PlowWow team · November 19, 2026**

After rain or a thaw, walk the route residents take from visitor parking to the lobby. A path can be clear of snow in the afternoon and still have a ribbon of ice across it by morning. Look for water crossing the route, where it gathers, and where it keeps running after the rest of the pavement dries.

Make the walk in daylight from a safe position. Bring a site map or open a photo on your phone that you can mark up. At each trouble spot, record where the water starts, where it crosses the route, and who can address it.

## 1. Follow downspout runoff across the sidewalk

Start with the downspouts nearest shared doors and sidewalks. Note where each outlet discharges. A downspout can empty beside a path while the grade carries water across it a few metres farther on. Look for a wet stripe or debris left in a line across the concrete.

Take one wide photo that shows the outlet and the crossing together. Mark the crossing on the map and send it to whoever handles building drainage. Ask the winter-service contact whether that section is in the treatment scope and who should be called if water continues to cross it overnight.

## 2. Check the low point at the parkade entrance

Look from a protected pedestrian area at the top and bottom of the ramp, the trench drain if there is one, and the threshold at the door. A pile beside the ramp may drain into the vehicle lane or onto a separate walking route. Check whether slush or debris is sitting over a grate and whether water is collecting before it reaches the drain.

Mark where the water pools and where pedestrians would encounter it. Have building maintenance assess drainage and send the same location to the winter crew so they can review the pile placement and agreed treatment area. If the ramp is busy or slippery, make the observation from the side and leave work in the travel lane to the site team.

## 3. Walk from visitor parking to the lobby

Check the corners behind retaining walls, the shaded side of the building, and the bottom of steps and curb ramps. Follow the route a visitor would take from the parking stall. A wet patch at the foot of a curb ramp can affect the only step-free route to the entrance even when the rest of the walkway looks dry.

Photograph the patch with a landmark visible, such as an entrance number or visitor stall. Identify the alternate accessible route if one exists and confirm who can put up signs or arrange a surface check before the next freeze. Include any side entrance that residents use early in the morning.

## Leave a note someone can act on

For each location, write down the time, a photo, and one sentence describing the flow. For example, “West lobby, 3:30 p.m. Downspout beside visitor stall 4 drains across the curb ramp toward the hedge.” Add the person responsible for the drainage follow-up, the contact for surface treatment, and when someone will check it again. Keep that note with this winter’s site map so a different manager or crew member can find the same spot.

When reviewing your winter-service scope, ask who takes a call between snowfalls, which routes are included, and who can approve an extra visit if one is needed. If you have a recurring puddle or refreeze spot, send PlowWow the property address and a marked photo when you [request a site review](/quote).
