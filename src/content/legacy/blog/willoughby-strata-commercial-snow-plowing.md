## The Realities of the Willoughby Microclimate

Willoughby sits on rolling upland terrain between the Nicomekl and Salmon River headwaters, higher and further from the moderating influence of the Fraser River than communities closer to the water. This elevation means Willoughby often records **colder overnight lows** than Langley City or Walnut Grove during the same storm system, extending the window during which fresh snow stays powdery before compacting.

The second factor is **new-construction drainage immaturity**. Because so much of Willoughby has been built within the last decade, landscaping, catch basins, and grading around many strata sites are still settling. Poorly graded new walkways and driveways can pool meltwater in unexpected low spots that weren't apparent until the first hard freeze exposes them.

## Navigating the Legal Landscape: Liability and Bylaws

Under the **BC Occupiers Liability Act**, every property owner or manager must take "reasonable care" to keep residents, visitors, and commuters safe on the premises. For a newly-formed strata corporation, this duty applies from day one — there is no grace period for a council still learning how its own property behaves in winter.

Willoughby falls under **Township of Langley** jurisdiction, where the **Township of Langley Traffic Bylaw** requires property owners and occupiers to clear snow and ice from adjacent sidewalks by **10:00 AM** the day following a snowfall. Non-compliance can bring fines and municipal clearing costs charged back to the property of **up to $1,000** per offence — a real budget risk for a young strata corporation still building its contingency reserve.
