## Why Shaughnessy Is the Most Demanding Snow-Removal Neighborhood in Vancouver

Shaughnessy is unlike any other Vancouver neighborhood when winter arrives. The combination of curving boulevards designed in 1907 by the Olmsted Brothers, century-old heritage mansions on quarter-acre lots, long sloped driveways carved through mature root zones, and First Shaughnessy Heritage Conservation Area restrictions on salt-aggressive de-icers — all of it adds up to a service profile that off-the-shelf snow contractors simply cannot handle. PlowWow has been clearing First, Second, and Third Shaughnessy properties since our founding, and we built our equipment fleet and chemistry program specifically for the materials, landscaping, and access constraints unique to this neighborhood.

When snow arrives on the south slope of Vancouver's west side, Shaughnessy sees it first and keeps it longest. The neighborhood sits roughly 80 metres above sea level on the crest between False Creek and the Fraser River, which means temperatures here run 1–2°C cooler than Kitsilano or Marpole below. A storm that drops wet slush on Granville Island will leave 8–12 cm of dry, compactable snow on The Crescent. That snow then refreezes overnight on stamped concrete driveways, slate walkways, and porte-cochère entries — surfaces that are expensive to repair and impossible to repour quickly under heritage rules.

## Salt, Chloride, and Heritage Conservation

First Shaughnessy properties carry heritage conservation designations that affect more than just architecture — many landscape elements (specimen trees, original stone walls, lead-paned greenhouses) are protected too. Sodium chloride (rock salt) splash damages limestone, sandstone, and historic brick mortar. Calcium chloride brine damages bronze fixtures and patinated copper downspouts. Magnesium chloride is gentler but still toxic to mature root systems in concentrations above ~30 g/m².

PlowWow's Shaughnessy de-icing program uses a tiered chemistry approach. Driveways and parking pads get a calcium-magnesium-acetate (CMA) blend that is safe for concrete and bronze. Walkways adjacent to heritage stonework get traction sand pre-applied before the storm, with a magnesium chloride brine applied only on the asphalt apron at the street. Stairs and entries get pet-safe potassium-formate granules. We document every application by location, product, and rate in the post-storm photo report sent to the property owner or landscape architect of record.

## Bylaws You Should Know

The City of Vancouver requires every property owner to clear snow and ice from the sidewalk adjacent to their property by 10:00 AM the day after a snowfall (Street and Traffic By-law 2849, Section 76). Heritage Conservation Area properties in First Shaughnessy carry additional restrictions on landscape disturbance during clearing. Pushing snow into the street or onto a neighbouring property is prohibited and can result in a $250 fine. PlowWow handles all sidewalk clearing on contracted properties and disposes of snow piles on-site in designated relocation zones (typically rear-yard turf or non-specimen bed corners agreed to in the pre-season walk-through).
