## Has New Westminster announced the Peterborough-style subsidy?

**We have not verified an approved equivalent in New Westminster as of October 8, 2026.** This article asks what a local trial could accomplish. It does not announce a grant, open registration or a change to the City’s sidewalk requirements.

Peterborough’s [new pilot](https://www.peterborough.ca/living-in-peterborough/roads-and-sidewalks/snow-removal/pilot-snow-removal-subsidy-program/) provides up to $450 toward private driveway clearing for eligible homeowners. It excludes condominiums where the corporation is responsible for snow removal. That distinction matters when discussing a possible New Westminster model.

Prompted by [Kawarthanow’s report](https://kawarthanow.com/2026/10/08/applications-open-october-15-for-city-of-peterboroughs-pilot-snow-removal-subsidy-program/), our local angle is the entire walking route: could assistance help someone get from their door to a usable sidewalk, rather than simply pay for the easiest area to clear?

## What New Westminster currently tells property owners

The [City’s snow-removal guidance](https://www.newwestcity.ca/services/streets-and-sidewalks/snow-removal) says owners and occupants must clear adjoining sidewalks by 10 a.m. on the day following a snowfall event. It encourages neighbours to adopt the sidewalk of a senior or a person with a mobility challenge. That encouragement should not be read as a guaranteed paid clearing service.

For help with municipal snow-removal questions, the page lists Public Works at **604-526-4691**. Ask staff about current assistance or referral options for the address. A public-road clearing schedule and help with a private entrance are separate questions.

Before arranging work, draw the route in short sections: doorway, steps or ramp, private path, frontage, crossing point and onward public route. Mark the party responsible for each section. A clear landing with an obstructed sidewalk connection still leaves the resident with a problem.

## A useful pilot would define the journey it supports

For a hypothetical sloped entrance, a contractor may need to do hand work that a truck cannot reach. For another property, the critical task may be keeping a gate and short path open. A locally designed pilot could compare those needs instead of using driveway area as the only measure of service.

That is a suggested design question, not evidence that New Westminster has adopted such a policy. Ask what a completed visit would mean: a measured entrance route, specified frontage, treatment of ice where suitable, and a way to report unfinished work. Record the surface and access conditions so the requested outcome is realistic.

A trial could also test simple booking support. A resident may need help obtaining a written scope or understanding who will return after further snow. Financial assistance and the ability to arrange service are related but different barriers.

## Keep apartment and strata responsibilities visible

People living in shared buildings may face access barriers even when they do not have a private driveway. That does not mean they qualify for a homeowner program elsewhere. It means the building needs a clear route plan and a contact who can authorize work.

A strata manager can identify the accessible entrance, paths to visitor or accessible parking, waste access and public frontage. Confirm the building’s actual maintenance responsibilities and contract; do not assume an individual resident is supposed to pay for common-property clearing. Our [New Westminster service information](/new-westminster) and [local strata planning guide](/new-westminster-strata-commercial-snow-plowing) provide a starting point for the work request.

At a park or public walkway boundary, confirm who maintains the connection. A neighbouring public destination does not put its paths into the strata’s contract, and clearing the building does not establish that the onward public route is open.

## Ask city hall for an assessment, with concrete questions

Contact the City through its [Mayor and Councillors page](https://www.newwestcity.ca/city_hall/mayor_and_council/). The City explains that correspondence relating to council matters can become part of the public meeting record. Keep a general policy request separate from personal eligibility documents.

Ask whether staff could assess:

1. Existing help for residents unable to clear their frontage or entrance.
2. Gaps affecting renters, seniors, people with disabilities and shared buildings.
3. A limited trial focused on a continuous usable route.
4. A fair way to select participants if demand exceeds the budget.
5. Provider availability, documented visits and a simple feedback process.

Include an example of the route that becomes blocked and the type of help missing. A diagram without identifying a vulnerable person may explain the issue better than a broad complaint about every street in the city.

## Arrange a winter plan while policy is discussed

For paid strata or commercial work, send photos, dimensions, steps, gate widths and access times through our [quote form](/quote). Ask what is included in a seasonal contract, five-visit option or single attendance. Specify clearing and any salt or appropriate brine treatment separately, with inspection and return-work responsibilities agreed in writing.

A possible subsidy is not a confirmed payment source. PlowWow does not administer it, promise eligibility or present a booking as reimbursable.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is this a New Westminster grant announcement?

No. It is an evidence-based discussion of a possible local approach. Confirm current programs directly with the City.

### Does clearing the parking lot cover the entrance path?

Only if the agreed scope includes it. Identify the pedestrian route and hand-work areas separately when requesting a quote.
