## Give each bidder the same Vancouver property brief

A fair contractor comparison starts with the same marked route. Include public sidewalk frontages, building entrances, pedestrian connections and any vehicle work. Downtown mixed-use properties may have loading access and opening hours to consider; a smaller West Side strata may have a short frontage but awkward access. Explain the property rather than treating those examples as fixed rate categories.

## Confirm the sidewalk plan

The City of Vancouver’s winter guidance says owners and occupants are responsible for removing snow and ice from the full width of adjacent sidewalks by 10 a.m. each day when snow or freezing conditions are present. A corner lot also needs its side frontage considered. Discuss that requirement with the contractor and establish a service plan the contractor can accept. Reading a city rule does not create a guaranteed attendance commitment in an unsigned quote.

## Ask what happens after the first pass

A cleared route can need another assessment as conditions change. Ask who monitors the property, how additional treatment is decided and how residents report a missed area. Identify drains, runoff and storage constraints in advance. Request a clear record of visits and authorized work. The proposal should distinguish a clearing pass, additional salt-only treatment and separately approved relocation or hauling.

## Check capacity, contact and contract

Discuss local route coverage, suitable equipment, crew backup and access if parked vehicles block the work. Request current insurance information and relevant references. Compare the seasonal total before GST and its inclusions rather than only the monthly payment. Keep the accepted boundaries, service trigger, payment dates and extra-work process together so council can explain the choice to owners.

A reviewed Vancouver quote can turn the property brief into a defined proposal. Tell us whether you need strata common-area work, commercial access or a residential route assessment.
