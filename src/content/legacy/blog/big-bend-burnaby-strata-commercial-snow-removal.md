## What Makes Big Bend a Different Snow Job

Big Bend is not a strata neighbourhood. It's a working district, and every snow decision has to reflect that. Six factors make it unlike anything else in Burnaby:

- **Fraser River cold pooling.** Cold air drains off the North Shore mountains and pools along the Fraser River lowlands overnight. Big Bend routinely runs 2–3 °C colder than the Burnaby Metrotown reading — meaning ice forms here first and lingers longest.
- **Pre-6 a.m. shift starts.** Food-processing, cold-storage and dock operations run staggered shifts starting at 4:00, 5:00 and 6:00 a.m. A yard cleared at 8:00 a.m. is a yard cleared after the shift lost.
- **Refrigerated logistics.** Yards handle 53-foot reefers on tight radii. Uncleared aprons and iced-over dock plates mean product loss, not just inconvenience.
- **Greenhouse and agricultural operations.** Marine Way, Byrne Road and Marine Drive host greenhouse operations where propane and biomass deliveries have to reach the site regardless of weather.
- **Long private drive aisles.** Big Bend sites often run 200+ metres of private aisle from Marine Way to a rear yard. That's a substantial plow scope, and the aisle needs to be clear before the first truck rolls.
- **Fraser Foreshore Park adjacency.** Public trails along the river run through and around some Big Bend industrial frontages. Sites with adjacent public trails carry both bylaw-clearing exposure and public-safety exposure.

## Occupiers Liability and WorkSafeBC in an Industrial Setting

An industrial landlord or operator in Big Bend carries three overlapping duties on a snow morning:

1. **Occupiers Liability Act (BC).** The occupier owes a duty of care to anyone the occupier permits on the premises — drivers, contractors, vendors, delivery personnel and public visitors.
2. **WorkSafeBC obligations for the operator.** Employees walking to a shift, moving between buildings, or working on outdoor yards must have a safe surface. Slip incidents inside the site perimeter are investigated as workplace incidents.
3. **Contract carrier expectations.** Reefer and dry-goods carriers who take delivery on your site can, and do, refuse to unload on unsafe surfaces. That refusal cascades into supply chain problems no amount of after-the-fact clearing solves.

In practice, this means an industrial snow contract has to specify:

- Pre-shift finish time (4:30 or 5:30 a.m., depending on operations)
- Yard, apron and dock-plate treatment
- Fire lane and hydrant access
- Scale-house and gatehouse approaches
- Pedestrian paths between buildings
- Public-facing sidewalk and trail frontage under bylaw

Anything less is under-scoped for Big Bend.

## Seasonal vs Per-Event — The Industrial Math

For a Big Bend operator, the case for seasonal is even sharper than for a strata:

1. **A missed pre-shift is a lost shift.** For a 50-employee cold-storage operation, a lost half-shift is a five-figure hit. Per-event contractors are structurally incapable of guaranteeing pre-shift clears — they service seasonal customers first.
2. **Reefer downtime compounds.** Idling refrigerated trucks in a queue while a yard is being cleared burns fuel, hits ELD hours and generates carrier surcharges. Per-event models make that queue longer.
3. **Insurance renewals reward documentation.** Industrial CGL and property insurers ask for winter maintenance documentation at renewal. A seasonal contract with GPS and photo logs is the answer they want.
4. **CAM tenant relationships.** Multi-tenant industrial parks recover snow through CAM. Seasonal contracts produce a predictable, defensible line item. Per-event surprise invoices trigger tenant disputes.

Across three seasons of Big Bend industrial quotes, the seasonal contract has come in equal to or cheaper than a "typical" per-event winter, and dramatically cheaper than a hard one.

### Are dock plates and dock aprons part of the base scope?

Yes. Dock plates, dock aprons and container-turn radii are in scope on every Big Bend industrial contract. These are the surfaces where reefer refusal-to-unload and workplace slip incidents actually happen, so treating them as extras would defeat the purpose of the contract.

### Do you maintain fire lane and hydrant access during snow events?

Yes. Fire lane and hydrant access is maintained every event. Loss of fire lane access on an industrial site is a Fire Code violation and a WorkSafeBC exposure — it isn't optional.

### What de-icer product do you use in industrial yards?

A disclosed chloride blend sized to the surface — magnesium chloride and CMA on pedestrian paths and building entrances, standard rock salt or a treated chloride blend on drive aisles and yards where pedestrian exposure is controlled. Application rate is logged per visit.

Per-visit GPS breadcrumbs, timestamped photos of key surfaces, product and rate, technician on site and time, weekly summaries, and an end-of-season package designed to satisfy CGL renewal requirements, CAM reconciliation, and internal WorkSafeBC / safety audits.

### Do you clear the Fraser Foreshore Park trail frontage adjacent to my site?

Where the trail frontage is on the property's clearing perimeter under bylaw, yes — it's part of the base scope. Public trails through and adjacent to industrial frontages carry both bylaw and public-safety exposure, and treating them as extras isn't defensible.
