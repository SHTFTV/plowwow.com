# First Frost, Fraser Valley: Protect Concrete and Plan Ice Control

![ PlowWow planning diagram showing concrete identification, approved traction materials and repeat route inspections](/blog-images/first-frost-fraser-valley-concrete-ice-control.jpg "PlowWow first-frost planning diagram")

## Frost on the ground? Time to get ready

Langley, Abbotsford, Maple Ridge, Mission and Aldergrove property managers: have you secured your winter supplies and arranged who will check the site? A frosty morning is the reminder to review entrances and walking routes before the winter rush. If your building or concrete is new, the treatment plan deserves particular attention. This is a seasonal reminder prompted by a local frost observation, not a claim of confirmed frost across every community.

## Protect the finish before choosing the product

Send us the concrete age, installer instructions, finish type and any sealer details with your enquiry. New sidewalks and stamped entrance pads should be identified separately from asphalt and mature concrete. Concrete Saskatchewan recommends avoiding deicers during new concrete’s first winter and considering sand for traction. A product advertised as concrete-safe should not be treated as approval for a new slab or decorative coating. The installer’s care directions and product restrictions matter.

## Stock the right supplies for the right surface

The goal is to secure materials for an approved plan, rather than buying whichever bag is left when conditions change. Confirm suitable traction material for surfaces where deicer use is restricted, keep it dry and clearly labelled, and assign someone to check stock. Sand adds traction but does not melt ice. It can move or become covered, so it needs follow-up attention. Ask who replenishes it and who checks the affected route.

## Plowing and frost response are different needs

A parking lot may need equipment access and snow storage while an entrance needs hand clearing and repeat condition checks. Tell us which pedestrian routes, steps, ramps and doors are priorities and when people use them. Include shaded areas, low points and water draining across the path. Do not assume that a snowfall-based service trigger automatically includes every frost-only morning or follow-up visit; those details belong in the proposed scope.

## Flag stamped concrete and recent pours on the map

A simple marked photograph can show what a crew needs to recognize before snow covers it. Identify decorative edges, new concrete, pavers, curbs, drain covers and any existing damage. Ask the contractor and installer to confirm suitable clearing methods and equipment boundaries. Sealing is not a universal permission to apply salt, and aggressive scraping can damage a sensitive surface. Keep the restrictions available for every person who works on the site.

## Plan for wet weather and the next freeze

Winter readiness includes drainage and inspections, not just keeping a shovel and a bag in storage. Note puddles, downspouts discharging onto paths and proposed snow piles that could send meltwater toward an entrance. Agree on who checks those spots when conditions change and how urgent concerns are reported. If a route is unsafe, arrange a safer alternative and notify the responsible person rather than relying on the previous visit’s completion report.
