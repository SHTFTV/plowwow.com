## Match the plan to the Burnaby property

A Brentwood mixed-use entrance, a Metrotown parking area and a Big Bend commercial lot do not have the same clearing task. Describe the pedestrian connections, circulation lanes, loading points and requested stalls. Identify any sloped approach or parkade ramp as explicit scope. For elevated locations, discuss site exposure rather than automatically borrowing a lower-route winter allowance.

## Choose equipment for the access

Open lanes may suit a truck and plow, while tight stalls or limited turning space can change the equipment requirement. Tell the contractor whether stalls must be recovered, when vehicles can move and whether dedicated machinery is requested. Storage locations should preserve access and drainage. Later relocation and trucking are different work from routine pushing into an agreed pile.

## Separate clearing from additional ice treatment

A continuing storm may require several passes. Ice treatment can also be needed without new snow, so the agreement should explain the monitoring arrangement, service trigger and additional attendance. Count the treatment included in a snow-and-salt visit once, and identify extra salt-only allowances separately. Material selection and application should follow the agreed surface and conditions.

## Compare the full winter commitment

Request the seasonal total before GST, service dates, payment schedule and clear terms for limits or extraordinary weather. Ask how backup equipment and local crew availability support the proposal. The City of Burnaby publishes sidewalk-clearing guidance that should be considered in the site plan.
