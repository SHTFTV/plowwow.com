## Which Langley sidewalk deadline applies?

First confirm whether the property is in Langley City or the Township of Langley. In the City, bordering sidewalks and walkways must be cleared within 24 hours of snowfall causing accumulation, or before snow exceeds 10 centimetres, whichever comes first. See [Langley City's winter guidance](https://www.langleycity.ca/city-services/roads-sidewalks/snow-ice-control).

The Township states that residential sidewalks and walkways must be cleared within 24 hours of first accumulation. For industrial, commercial and institutional properties, adjoining sidewalks and walkways must be cleared of snow and ice daily by 10 a.m. Check the [Township's requirements](https://www.tol.ca/en/services/snow-and-ice.aspx), including the property's applicable classification.

The address and jurisdiction should appear on the winter service request. A proposal headed simply “Langley” can leave the contractor and property manager working from different assumptions.

## Why this matters beyond the road

CBC's October 6, 2026 report by Nipun Tiwari describes Saint John increasing attention to sidewalk snow clearing. For Langley property managers, that raises a useful question: who keeps each pedestrian segment usable between a building, its frontage and a nearby public destination? The New Brunswick plan does not change local BC requirements.

A long townhouse path or commercial frontage can require considerable work even when the vehicle aisle is short. Assess pedestrian areas directly rather than estimating them as a minor addition to a parking-lot quote.

## A public park path may follow a different schedule

The Township publishes three priorities for hard-surface park trails and pathways. Routes connecting high pedestrian traffic to schools or bus stops, plus hilly or known icy areas, receive first-priority attention. Other routes have different service conditions, and third-priority work depends on resources after higher priorities are addressed.

That makes the actual connection important. A building beside a park should not assume every recreational path is maintained before residents leave in the morning. Check the particular route with the municipality and distinguish it from private paths and required frontage work.

For a hypothetical townhouse development with a gate opening toward a park, label three areas on the plan: the internal approach, the boundary connection and the municipal trail. Identify the responsible party for each. A private contractor's scope should not silently extend into municipal land, and a municipal trail schedule should not substitute for the strata's own entrance plan.

## Measure a continuous route through the property

Start at the entrance used by residents or customers and trace the path to the public sidewalk. Record gates, bollards, turns, steps and changes in surface. Include the narrowest opening and the space available for turning equipment. A machine that fits the broad middle section may not reach the final doorway.

Map snow-storage areas before assigning equipment. Keep the walking route connected as work progresses and agree how to handle occupied stalls or delivery traffic. For larger commercial properties, separate the walkway crew's tasks from loader or truck work. ## Build the deadline into a workable agreement

Ask the contractor to identify the included surfaces and applicable frontage deadline. Then specify the private routes, opening hours, inspection responsibility and process for further attendance. Meeting a municipal clearing deadline should not be described as a guarantee that conditions will remain unchanged afterward.

Record who approves relocation, what happens if access is blocked and how the contractor reports unfinished areas. For multiple buildings, use named entrances and marked segments so completion notes are understandable to the manager receiving them.

## Choose a booking option with clear limits

Request a seasonal scope if the property needs an ongoing response arrangement. If asking for five visits, confirm how combined plowing and treatment are counted and whether materials or return visits are additional. A single visit may address a defined need, subject to availability, but it does not establish ongoing monitoring by itself.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Are Langley City and Township snow rules the same?

No. The City uses a 24-hour or 10-centimetre threshold. The Township distinguishes residential timing from the daily 10 a.m. requirement for industrial, commercial and institutional properties.

### Does the Township clear every park pathway first thing in the morning?

No blanket commitment applies to every path. Its published policy assigns priorities and makes lower-priority work dependent on conditions and available resources.

### What should a strata include when requesting a quote?

Provide the exact jurisdiction, address, route plan, gate widths, surface details, access hours and snow-storage options. Identify monitoring and return-service expectations separately.
