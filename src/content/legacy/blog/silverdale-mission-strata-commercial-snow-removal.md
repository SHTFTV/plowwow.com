Silverdale is not a generic Mission neighbourhood — its microclimate, street grid and property mix shape how snow and ice have to be handled:

- **Fraser Valley cold pool.** Silverdale sits on the north bank of the Fraser at the west edge of Mission — cold outflow air from the Coquihalla and Fraser Canyon settles here overnight. Overnight lows run 3–5 °C colder than downtown Mission on outflow nights.
- **Sloped terrain.** Silverdale climbs from river-level dyke properties up to hillside stratas at 100+ metres elevation in a short distance. That gradient produces every winter surface type on one contract: freezing drizzle at the bottom, dry snow up top, glare ice on the slope in between.
- **Master-planned build-out.** The New Silverdale community is one of the Fraser Valley's largest active residential developments. Construction access, sidewalk hoardings and temporary crossings all extend the clearing perimeter beyond the strata footprint.
- **Long private driveways.** Many Silverdale hillside stratas share long, steep private access roads — these are common property under the BC Strata Property Act and carry the same duty-of-care exposure as any parking lot.
- **Bylaw exposure.** District of Mission bylaws require property owners to clear snow and ice from adjacent public sidewalks. Rural block faces make missed sections a direct life-safety issue for school walkers.
- **Freezing-rain and rapid refreeze.** Silverdale sees the classic Fraser Valley pattern — Pacific rain overrunning entrenched Arctic outflow — several times a winter, producing the worst glaze-ice events in Metro Vancouver.

## BC Strata Property Act Liability — What Silverdale Boards Need to Know

Under sections 3 and 72 of the *Strata Property Act*, a strata corporation is responsible for the repair and maintenance of common property. In case law across BC, that responsibility has been repeatedly interpreted to include a duty to keep sidewalks, walkways, parkade ramps and entrance courts reasonably free of snow and ice during winter events.

What this means in practice for a Silverdale strata council:

- **Documented service is the defense.** When a slip-and-fall claim is filed, the strata's insurer will ask for the service log — dispatch times, GPS breadcrumbs, salt-application photos, product used, technician on site. Without a written seasonal contract and per-visit documentation, the insurer's defense position collapses.
- **Per-event contractors create gaps.** A council that pays "as needed" is implicitly deciding when it thinks service is needed. That decision, made by a volunteer board on a Sunday night, is exactly the exposure plaintiffs' counsel targets.
- **Seasonal contracts move the decision.** With a PlowWow seasonal contract, the trigger threshold is defined in writing (typically 2 cm of accumulation or a forecast freezing-rain event), and dispatch is automatic. The council is no longer the decision-maker on a live event — the contract is.
- **Council minutes matter.** Every Silverdale strata board should record, in AGM minutes, that a seasonal snow contract with defined response times has been reviewed and approved. That paper trail is a meaningful part of any future defense.
- **Adjacent bylaw exposure.** District of Mission Traffic and Highway Regulation Bylaw obliges the owner or occupier of adjacent property to clear public sidewalks of snow and ice within a reasonable time after accumulation. That obligation runs with the property whether or not the strata has hired a contractor.

## Credentials to Verify Before Signing Any Silverdale Contract

Before a strata council or commercial landlord in Silverdale signs a snow contract with anyone, verify:

- **WorkSafeBC (WCB) clearance letter.** No clearance letter, no contract — full stop. Under the *Workers Compensation Act*, the property owner can be assessed for the contractor's unpaid WCB premiums if a worker is injured on site.
- **Commercial general liability insurance.** Minimum $5,000,000, with the strata corporation or landlord named as additional insured. Ask for the certificate.
- **Automobile liability.** Every plow truck and salt unit needs commercial auto coverage.
- **Written service specification.** Trigger threshold (cm), response time from trigger, surfaces included, de-icer product, documentation delivered.
- **De-icer product disclosure.** Rock salt (NaCl), calcium chloride, magnesium chloride, calcium magnesium acetate — each has different pet, plant and concrete impacts. Get the answer in writing.
- **Subcontractor policy.** Some low-price bidders route your site to whichever subcontractor is closest that morning. Ask whether the crew on your site will be direct employees or subs — and whether subs carry the same WCB and CGL coverage.
