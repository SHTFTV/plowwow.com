## What the supplied November analysis does—and does not—say

Severe Weather Europe’s October 28 analysis discusses model projections of an unusually early polar-vortex disruption in November. A forecast split or wind reversal is not an observed record and does not establish snowfall at a Lower Mainland address. NOAA’s October ENSO discussion also describes a strengthening El Niño. Treat both as large-scale context; use current local forecasts and site conditions for an operating decision.

## A preparation cue, not a city snow forecast

CTV News reported on October 28 that meteorologists are watching a possible early weakening of the polar vortex, with the outcome for Canada unresolved. That is a reason to review winter readiness, not a prediction of a particular storm. This Surrey guide is an original planning checklist. It does not predict local snowfall, a start date or the severity of this winter.

## Do not treat every part of a site as one job

A parking or work yard, a public-facing walkway and a narrow entrance can require different methods. Mark each work area and state what must stay open. Describe surface conditions and obstacles without assuming that a large machine is suitable everywhere.

## Check municipal guidance and contract scope

Surrey’s official winter-safety guidance asks owners to clear snow and ice from adjoining sidewalks by 10 a.m. daily. Read the current city guidance and confirm which boundaries are included in your service discussion. A municipal plow route and a private-property agreement do different jobs.

## Give equipment and people separate roles

Ask who handles machine clearing, who handles hand work and who checks an inaccessible area. Identify a backup contact for access problems. If equipment service is needed, send the machine identity and symptoms to the service provider; do not expect a weather enquiry to diagnose a hydraulic or electrical fault.

## Make an enquiry that can be reviewed

Provide the Surrey address, site type, access window and a marked list of requested tasks. Explain existing commitments and restrictions before discussing timing. Do not infer an available crew, a fixed quote or an accepted service territory from a city-specific article. Those details require a direct confirmation.

## Use a current forecast for the actual decision

ECCC’s seasonal guidance concerns longer-period probabilities, not an address-specific attendance time. Recheck local forecasts and warnings as an event approaches. Ask PlowWow to review the property and confirm scope, availability and commercial terms before relying on a service arrangement.
