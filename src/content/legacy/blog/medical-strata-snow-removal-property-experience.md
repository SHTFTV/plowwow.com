# Winter Access Matters: Our Medical and Strata Property Experience

**By the PlowWow team · October 21, 2026**

A snowy morning does not cancel everyone’s day. People still have medical appointments, staff still need to get to work, and residents still need a usable route from their front door. At a busy property, winter service has to be planned around those everyday movements.

We are proud of the medical buildings and strata communities we have worked at, and the property contacts who help coordinate that work. Our experience includes Columbia Medical Building in New Westminster, Wilson Medical in Port Coquitlam, and established strata properties in Burnaby and Vancouver. Each has its own entrances, vehicle access and pedestrian routes. A sensible winter plan starts by understanding those differences.

## Medical buildings: people need to get there

Columbia Medical Building is across from Royal Columbian Hospital in New Westminster. That setting puts the importance of winter access into perspective. People arriving for care may be worried, uncomfortable or less steady on their feet. A path that looks like a small part of a parking plan can be the most important part of their visit.

Our service experience at Columbia Medical is joined by confirmed seasonal work at Wilson Medical in Port Coquitlam. Both medical properties are part of our 2026–27 winter work through our service coordination arrangements.

The practical questions are straightforward. Where do people enter? Which pedestrian routes connect the parking area to those doors? Where does a vehicle cross a walkway? What time does the building begin receiving people, and how will the property contact report a changing condition?

Those questions deserve attention before the first snowfall. They help establish the agreed priority areas and the work that needs a separate conversation. Medical use makes careful planning especially relevant; it does not turn a contractor’s service into a guarantee that every surface will always be free of ice.

## Strata experience: the whole route matters

Our past strata work includes The Embassy in Burnaby, University Gate in Vancouver’s UBC area, and a long-standing East Georgia strata property in Vancouver. These properties are part of our work in previous seasons; each winter’s renewal is confirmed separately.

At The Embassy, our scope has included the circular driveway, the ramp and smaller sidewalks. At University Gate and the East Georgia property, the pedestrian work includes front and rear routes and internal shared sidewalks. These are different jobs even when the properties appear similar in a quick aerial view.

A ramp brings a different access problem from a level path. A rear entrance may matter as much to residents as the main door. A stretch of internal sidewalk can be easy to overlook if a proposal only says “frontage.” Experience is useful when it leads to a clearer conversation about the actual route people use.

We have coordinated strata work with contacts at **Ascent Real Estate Management Corporation** and **Citybase Management Ltd.**, alongside **JLS Master Gardeners Inc.** in our service coordination. These relationships are built around specific properties and seasons, with clear communication between the building’s contacts and our winter service team.

## A winter plan should follow the property

For a medical building, strata community or commercial property, we want the work described clearly enough that the property contact and the crew are talking about the same surfaces. A useful site review covers:

- **Pedestrian access:** agreed entrances, connecting paths, steps, landings and shared sidewalks.
- **Vehicle access:** the drive, parking areas, turning space and any ramp that needs separate review.
- **Operating needs:** arrival times, deliveries and areas that must remain available during service.
- **Snow placement:** where cleared snow can go without obstructing an entrance, drain, sightline or neighbouring property.
- **Communication:** who can approve additional work and how a changing condition is reported.

This is why we prefer a property-specific scope over a vague promise to “do the lot.” A smaller site can still have several important transitions, while a larger open area may be more straightforward to clear with equipment. Size is part of the price; access, layout and the agreed service obligations matter too.

## Snow clearing and ice control need their own conversation

A snow-clearing visit and an ice-control visit are different pieces of winter work. The written proposal should say what each includes, which surfaces are covered and how additional visits are handled. Clearing once does not stop new precipitation, runoff or a later freeze from changing conditions.

Our online starting prices apply to **normal-condition snowfall events within the stated scope**. Deep accumulation, prolonged storms, restricted access, hauling, ramps and additional equipment can change the work and require a separate agreement. A seasonal arrangement also needs its service dates, visit terms and extraordinary-weather provisions in writing.

We would rather explain those details before winter than leave a property manager guessing after the weather changes. Read our [Lower Mainland snow removal pricing guide](/snow-removal-pricing-lower-mainland/) for the broader pricing discussion.

## Clear communication is part of the service

Property managers and councils need to know who is responsible for what. Our aim is a practical relationship: agree the scope, identify the important routes and keep a clear point of contact. Site-specific timing and reporting arrangements belong in that agreement rather than a blanket promise made to every visitor on a website.

If your building has a known problem area, tell us early. A ramp, shaded landing or narrow connection between entrances can change how we approach the site. The same goes for access restrictions and places where machinery will not fit. Photographs and a marked site plan can make the discussion more useful, with private operational details kept out of public posts.
