## Give your rental property a place on a planned winter route

Seasonal service starts with preparation. PlowWow reviews the property, the equipment and people needed, and how the work fits an available route before accepting a booking. That gives the owner or property manager an opportunity to settle the details before a storm creates competing demands.

A confirmed seasonal agreement sets out the service dates, included areas, snow and ice work, pricing and attendance terms. Ask how repeat visits, treatment between snowfalls and exceptional weather are handled. The written agreement should make clear what you are booking and how any additional work is approved.

For an occupied rental, those details also make communication easier. You can explain the agreed service to the property contact and give tenants a clear way to report an access concern.

## Start with the routes your tenants actually use

A rental property can have several different winter access needs. Identify each area you want reviewed:

- **Entrances and shared walkways:** the route from the sidewalk or parking area to the building.
- **Steps and landings:** stairs along the agreed common route, plus any separately requested unit access.
- **Driveways and parking areas:** vehicle entrances, drive lanes and the specific spaces included in the proposed work.
- **Public sidewalk frontage:** the frontage you want included in the service scope.
- **Ramps, gates and other access points:** locations that need a separate review of clearance, slope or equipment access.

A marked photograph or simple site plan is a useful starting point. Show where vehicles are usually parked, which gates are locked and where snow could be stored without blocking an entrance or a route people need.

Individual-unit paths, extra clearing areas and off-site snow hauling should be discussed explicitly. They can change the work and the price.

## One rental home or a managed portfolio

For a single rental home, name the person who can coordinate access and receive service updates. If you live elsewhere, confirm who can answer a site question when the crew is working.

For an apartment building or a portfolio of rental properties, send the addresses separately with the contact and requested areas for each one. Buildings that look similar can have different entrances, parking arrangements and priorities. A separate scope for each address helps everyone understand which work belongs where.

Keep tenants informed about agreed parking or gate arrangements. Tell the site contact when access changes so the service plan can be reviewed.
