## A long access driveway changes the work description

A standard residential example is a single driveway and walkway. It is not a blanket price for a long access lane, acreage, several buildings or an equipment yard. Describe the length, usable clearing width, gates, turning area and snow-storage space. If access has to remain open while the work is done, explain that requirement before the price is finalised.

## City and Township sidewalk guidance differ

Langley City’s published guidance describes removal within 24 hours after snowfall or before accumulation exceeds 10 cm. The Township describes a 24-hour requirement for residential frontage and daily 10 am clearance for industrial, commercial and institutional frontage. Check the applicable municipal guidance and property classification when agreeing the service plan.

## Shared paths deserve a marked route

For townhouse or mixed-use property, trace the common route from parking and public frontage to the buildings. Note connecting steps, bins, mailboxes and any areas requested outside common property. Tell us which paths the strata expects to remain accessible and who can authorise extra work. The cost follows the agreed work; a number of doors is not a substitute for a site plan.

## Choose a booking model before comparing the totals

A seasonal proposal should explain the winter dates and service obligations. A five-visit arrangement should define what counts as a visit, while an on-demand request needs an availability check. Custom plans are available to discuss. A lower headline figure is only useful when access, materials, repeat work and extraordinary-weather terms match the property’s needs.

The 2026/27 season means winter 2026 into 2027. It does not mean a second 2027/28 season is included. Seasonal, five-visit and custom arrangements must have their own dates, scope and terms.
