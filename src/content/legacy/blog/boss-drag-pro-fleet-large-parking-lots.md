## Why we're adding a wide rear plow

A rear drag plow pulls snow as the truck moves forward. The DRAG PRO's folding wings let the operator adjust the working configuration for the space. BOSS lists this model at **8 feet folded and 16 feet fully extended**. That maximum width is useful context for an open lot; it isn't a promise of a constant 16-foot clearing path around parked cars, islands or narrow access.

On its [exact-model product page](https://www.bossplow.com/en/product/RPL21616), BOSS says the expanding wings let operators move more snow in each pass. Its [rear-plow intended-use guide](https://info.bossplow.com/blog/drag-pro-rear-plow-intended-uses) describes wider passes and adjustable wings for quicker parking-aisle cleanup. Those are manufacturer-described benefits, and they're the practical reason this addition interests us: put more of the truck to work and make efficient use of suitable open pavement.

This is the DRAG PRO 8–16 hydraulic folding wing model, **RPL21616**. Today's news is the planned fleet addition; installation completion and operating results will follow separately.

## Strip malls, retail plazas and larger commercial lots

A strip mall needs more than a clear rectangle of asphalt. Customer parking, storefront access, delivery areas and a sensible place to put the snow all matter. We want to hear from property managers with broad parking areas where a wide rear-pull setup could fit the clearing plan.

When you request a review, include the approximate **paved area to be cleared**, rather than only the total property size. These are useful ways to describe the job:

- **Around 10,000 square feet:** tell us how much is parking versus drive aisles, and where cars remain overnight. Access and turning room need a close look.
- **Around 25,000–50,000 square feet:** flag storefront rows, islands, loading bays and the snow-storage area so the clearing sequence can be reviewed.
- **Around 100,000 square feet or more:** send a site plan if you have one, with priority entrances, delivery traffic, storage space and any areas requiring separate equipment.

These sizes help describe an enquiry; they aren't equipment eligibility thresholds or clearance-time estimates. We assess each property and route before confirming a proposal.

## How much time can a rear plow save?

BOSS publishes a clear efficiency case: wider passes, more snow moved per pass and flexible parking-aisle cleanup. Its intended-use guide specifically discusses reducing cleanup time on small to medium parking lots. **The BOSS sources linked here do not provide measured average minutes saved for 10,000-, 50,000- or 100,000-square-foot lots.** We won't turn that qualitative benefit into an unsupported percentage or a promised finish time for your property.

The full job includes manoeuvring around cars, pulling snow away from edges, moving and stacking it, entrances and any separately agreed walkway or de-icing work. Snow depth and weight also affect the operation. BOSS cautions that rear plows are not intended to break down frozen snowbanks or plow frozen snow. A site review is how we match the equipment and service scope to the work you actually need.
