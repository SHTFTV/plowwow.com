## Navigating the Legal Landscape: Liability and Bylaws

Under the **BC Occupiers Liability Act**, every property owner or manager must take "reasonable care" to keep people on the premises safe — a standard that applies as much to a loading dock worker as it does to a strata resident. Courts consistently treat the absence of a documented, professional snow removal agreement as a gap in that duty of care.

Locally, Port Kells falls under Surrey jurisdiction, where the **City of Surrey Traffic Bylaw, 1997, No. 13007** requires property owners and occupiers to clear snow and ice from adjacent sidewalks and walkways by **10:00 AM** the day following a snowfall. Non-compliance can bring fines of **up to $500**, and in a commercial or industrial setting, an uncleared dock apron or forklift lane multiplies both the safety risk and the legal exposure.

## Overcoming Common Operational Pitfalls

Managing a Port Kells industrial property involves more than pushing snow off pavement — freight schedules and heavy vehicle traffic introduce failure points a residential route rarely sees.

### Loading Dock Ramp Compaction
The constant traffic of forklifts and trailers across a dock ramp compresses fresh snow into a hard-packed sheet within minutes. If de-icer isn't applied before the first truck arrives, that compacted layer becomes a slip and skid hazard for the rest of the day.

### Trailer Court Refreeze
Port Kells' river-corridor humidity means meltwater lingers on trailer court pavement longer than in drier municipalities. When temperatures drop overnight, that moisture refreezes into sheet ice across parking stalls and drive lanes.
