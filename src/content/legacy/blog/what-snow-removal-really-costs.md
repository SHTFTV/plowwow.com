## How do I know what a snow-removal contract is really worth?

Start with the work being purchased, not a price from a neighbouring building. The useful comparison is the same property plan, service period and response terms. A small frontage, a steep ramp and a large parking lot involve different work even when their addresses are close together.

Ask for the covered areas, clearing and ice-control scope, visit triggers, return arrangements, extra-work rules and taxes in writing. A cheaper total can cover less work. A higher total is not automatically better service. The contractor should explain what the price buys and what would change it.

## Can I find an approximate price online?

An estimate can help you explore a budget when its assumptions are visible. Measure the areas, describe your property, identify ramps and access restrictions, and say what service you need. Default figures in a form are not measurements of your site.

PlowWow currently accepts enquiries through its [quote-request page](/quote). Our private pricing worksheet is a prototype, not a published instant-price service or a confirmed rate card. Its assumptions still need commercial and property review. We are not publishing the worksheet’s example rates as local market averages.

## What is the difference between seasonal and individual-event pricing?

A fixed seasonal package reserves an agreed scope for an agreed period under written terms. The price also supports planning and capacity, rather than simply reimbursing a truck each time it moves. Ask what is included and what happens in exceptional conditions. A fixed price does not automatically mean unlimited work.

A per-visit price applies to a defined attendance or operation. Establish whether an “event” means one dispatch, a snowfall, one treatment, or several visits during continuing accumulation. A snowstorm and a billable service event are not necessarily the same thing. Clearing, salting and follow-up work need their own definitions.

## Can I book only while I am away?

Ask about a defined coverage period before leaving: start and end dates, property areas, the services requested, access and a contact who can make decisions. Ask whether a small event pack, scheduled assessment or temporary arrangement is available for your property. Dates, route capacity, acceptance and payment terms must be confirmed; submitting an enquiry does not reserve a crew.

Agree how a visit is triggered while you are away. Should the provider monitor conditions, attend only after an agreed trigger, or respond to an authorized request? Who receives updates? Who approves work beyond the allowance? Do not assume that a five-event pack includes continuous monitoring, every refreeze or unlimited return visits. Keep private travel dates and entry instructions within the service conversation.

## How much does one snow event cost?

There is no dependable single figure without the property and scope. A truck clearing open parking, a hand crew working stairs and an ice-only visit are different operations. Ask for the minimum charge, included work, expected attendance and extra-work rates before accepting an on-call visit.

PlowWow’s published package comparison describes five-event pricing at 20% above the comparable seasonal per-service baseline, with on-call pricing a further 20% above the five-pack rate. That is 44% above the original baseline, not 40%. These are relative comparisons for equivalent scope, not a dollar tariff, a seasonal total divided by five, or a promise that an on-call booking will be available. Check the current proposal and written terms.

## What makes a heavy event cost more?

Depth alone does not describe the job. Continued accumulation, wet or compacted snow, drifting, traffic, restricted access and limited storage can increase clearing time. A crew may need repeated passes, different equipment or a separate relocation decision. Salt is not a substitute for removing a substantial accumulation.

The contract should explain what counts as extraordinary work, how it is measured, what remains included and how any additional charge is calculated. A depth band, additional crew time and a snow-hauling item can be useful ways to describe scope, but charging all three for the same work without explanation invites confusion.

## How should the calculator handle heavy snow?

Our existing private worksheet does not yet calculate heavy-event work, equipment-hour budgets or material quantities. Its seasonal result must not be represented as covering those items automatically. A stronger version should show a normal-scope estimate and a separately itemized exceptional-event allowance.

The planning structure is: **included work + authorized additional equipment/crew hours + additional measured material + agreed transport or hauling − work already covered**. A multiplier is only useful if its threshold and included work are explained. It should not silently multiply taxes, duplicate a mobilization charge or compound another charge for the same extra hours.

For a seasonal agreement, calculate an exceptional-event change only after checking the included allowance. For a one-off booking, confirm the actual conditions and quoted basis before dispatch. Illustrative calculator inputs are not permission to add a charge to a customer’s contract.

## What goes into an hourly equipment charge?

An equipment billing rate needs to identify the machine and what comes with it. Internal operating costs can include ownership or lease costs, maintenance, fuel, insurance, attachments and an operator. The customer rate may also support overhead, readiness and margin. Those are different from a universally published market price.

Ask for a line for each proposed resource: hand crew per person-hour or crew-hour; blower or compact sidewalk equipment; truck with blade; salter or brine unit; skid steer; loader or backhoe; and hauling trucks when needed. Confirm whether labour, fuel and attachments are already included. Do not add a separate operator or fuel charge for the same cost unless the quote explicitly excludes it from the machine rate.

## How do minimum hours, truck charges and mobilization work?

A dispatch minimum may be a floor applied to the visit subtotal, or a separately identified travel/mobilization fee. Those are different structures. The quote should say which applies, when billing starts, the minimum billed time, rounding increments and whether travel or waiting is chargeable.

An equipment minimum can also affect a small job. Estimated working time should be compared with the stated minimum once, not repeatedly for the same resource. If two machines are required, identify why and show their respective time. Transporting a loader to site is different from operating it there; make any transport charge explicit.

## How much salt will the site need?

There is no single safe application quantity that can be selected from air temperature alone. Clear Roads’ research guidance considers pavement temperature, its trend, surface conditions and the material used. WSDOT distinguishes pretreatment before a storm from de-icing after accumulation. Those highway sources explain the principles; their rates should not be copied directly onto a private walkway or a particular product.

The provider needs the actual treatment area, product instructions, surface suitability, weather, residual treatment and calibrated spreader output. Moisture can help activate solid salt, while rain, dilution, meltwater and further precipitation can change treatment needs. Lower temperatures can reduce effectiveness and change the appropriate approach; simply adding more product is not a universal answer.

## How should salt appear in the estimate?

Keep the physical quantity separate from the price. For granular material, an assessment might specify kilograms per defined area; for liquid, litres per defined area and the appropriate product concentration. Label units so nobody confuses a material application rate with a dollar-per-square-foot treatment charge.

Use **treated area ÷ reference area × assessed application rate** to plan quantity, then apply the agreed material and application pricing. The application rate must come from a suitable product-specific operating plan, not an automatic temperature slider. Record actual use and conditions during service. A temperature reading by itself does not establish the required quantity or guarantee an ice-free surface.

## Is an “eco”, pet-safe or concrete-safe upgrade automatically better?

Do not choose on the label alone. Ask which product is proposed, its intended temperature range, compatible surfaces, use instructions and limitations. New concrete, sensitive landscaping and pedestrian areas may need a different plan. No de-icer is universally harmless, and a percentage price premium is not evidence of suitability.

## What should I receive before saying yes?

Ask for a dated scope and property plan, the service period or defined visit, dispatch and equipment minimums, material basis, taxes, extras and payment schedule. Confirm who can authorize changes and how the provider reports blocked access, completed work and further needs. Keep the accepted version with subsequent updates and invoices.

Tell PlowWow your city, property type, measured areas, access, priorities and requested dates. Use the [current quote request](/quote) to discuss seasonal coverage, individual events or temporary needs. The useful outcome is an understandable offer for your property—not a low number detached from the work.
