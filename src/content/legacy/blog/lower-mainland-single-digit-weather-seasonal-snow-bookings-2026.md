**A 9°C air-temperature reading is a planning prompt, not an instruction to salt or a prediction of snow.** Actual treatment decisions depend on pavement conditions, moisture, the forecast and the agreed service plan.

## Seasonal contracts: agree on the whole winter plan

For strata councils, commercial property managers and businesses with repeated access needs, start with a site assessment and a written seasonal scope. Identify pedestrian entrances, accessible routes, parkade ramps, loading areas and parking aisles. Decide who monitors conditions, who authorizes work and what records you receive after a visit.

Ask what the contract includes: salting, brine where suitable, mechanical clearing, repeat attendance and snow relocation may have different terms. Confirm season dates, dispatch triggers, exclusions, material charges and any visit or accumulation limits. A seasonal agreement should make responsibilities clear before an overnight weather change.

## Five-visit bookings: define what counts as one visit

If you want a limited number of attendances, **ask for a five-visit option**. Specify whether you need five salting visits, five brine treatments where appropriate, or a proposed combination of plowing and salting.

Confirm whether plowing and salting at one attendance count together or separately, whether materials are included, how return visits are counted and when unused visits expire. Five visits should not be confused with five storms: one weather event may require more than one attendance. Price, scheduling and the final package depend on your property and the accepted quote.

## Single-use visits: salt, brine, or plowing plus salt

You can also **enquire about one-off service**. This may suit a specific need or a gap in an existing plan. Provide the address, current conditions, the areas needing attention and access instructions. A request is not a confirmed dispatch; wait for availability and scope confirmation.

- **Salting:** ask about treatment of agreed pedestrian and vehicle surfaces when conditions warrant it.
- **Brine:** ask whether the product, surface and expected weather make a liquid treatment appropriate. It is not a substitute for clearing accumulated snow.
- **Plowing plus salt:** identify the clearing areas, permitted snow-storage locations and the follow-up treatment requested. Walkways, occupied stalls and hauling need their own scope.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Should we order salt because the forecast says 9°C?

No. Single-digit air temperatures alone do not establish an icy surface or a need for treatment. Use this period to arrange service, and base attendance on actual conditions and your agreed plan.

### Can we ask for five visits instead of a full seasonal contract?

Yes. Enquire about five visits and identify the work you want included. Confirm visit counting, materials, expiry and scheduling in the written quote.

### Can we request just one salt, brine or plow-and-salt visit?

Yes. Send the address and scope for an availability check. Dispatch, product suitability and price require confirmation; submitting the enquiry does not book a crew automatically.
