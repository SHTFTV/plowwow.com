## Ironwood's Snow Profile — Why This Corner of Richmond Is Different

Ironwood isn't a standard Richmond retail node. Its geography and tenant mix produce a distinct set of winter risks:

- **Delta wind exposure.** With Highway 99 to the east and open Fraser delta land to the south, Ironwood catches the outflow winds off the Strait of Georgia. Those winds drop apparent temperatures 4–6 °C below the Environment Canada Richmond station reading — meaning ice forms here first.
- **Big-box open time pressure.** Home Depot, Save-On-Foods, Rona and the Ironwood Plaza anchors all open by 7 a.m. Lots must be cleared, curbed and salted before contractor trucks and commuter shoppers arrive.
- **Parkade + surface-lot combo sites.** Several Ironwood buildings pair below-grade parkades with open surface aprons. Ramp entrances hold black ice long after surface lots dry.
- **Cart returns and pedestrian islands.** Retail slip-and-fall claims cluster around cart corrals and painted pedestrian refuges — the exact spots plow trucks skip and salt spreaders under-cover.
- **Steveston Highway sidewalk runs.** Long, uninterrupted commercial sidewalk frontages create the highest bylaw-clearing exposure per lineal foot in south Richmond.
- **Adjacent townhome stratas.** Ironwood is ringed by townhome complexes on Ironwood Drive, Wireless Way and Steveston Highway. Their private lanes freeze faster than the arterial roads, and their entrance courts are the highest-injury surfaces in any strata.

## Retail Landlord Liability — Occupiers Liability Act and Bylaw Duty

Ironwood retail landlords carry two layers of winter exposure that a residential strata does not:

Under the *Occupiers Liability Act* (BC), an occupier owes a duty to take that care that in all the circumstances of the case is reasonable to see that a person on the premises will be reasonably safe. In retail case law, "reasonable" has consistently meant a documented pre-open clearing protocol on any surface where the public is invited.

Under Richmond Bylaw 8271 and successor consolidations, property owners are responsible for clearing public sidewalks adjacent to their property within a defined window after a snowfall — typically by 10 a.m. the day after the event. Missed sidewalks are the single most common ticketable offence in commercial districts during a Richmond snow event.

What this means for an Ironwood landlord or property manager:

- **The 7 a.m. threshold is not negotiable.** A contract that promises "morning" service without a written pre-open time is not defensible.
- **Photo documentation isn't optional.** Retail slip-and-fall plaintiffs subpoena the service log. If it doesn't exist, the defence starts on the back foot.
- **Cart corrals are surfaces too.** Any contract that clears "drive lanes" without naming corrals, refuges and pedestrian islands is under-scoped for a big-box site.

## The Seasonal Contract Case for Ironwood Retail

For an Ironwood big-box or strip-mall landlord, the per-event vs. seasonal decision usually comes down to three numbers:

1. **Pre-open guarantee.** Seasonal customers are cleared first. Per-event customers are cleared *after* seasonal — often at 10 a.m. instead of 6 a.m. On a big-box site, that four-hour gap is the difference between a routine morning and a claim.
2. **Freezing-rain dispatches.** Metro Vancouver's most dangerous mornings are rarely fresh accumulation — they're refreeze after a Fraser delta fog. Per-event contracts don't dispatch on those forecasts. Seasonal contracts do.
3. **CAM recovery predictability.** Retail landlords recover snow costs through common-area maintenance (CAM). Seasonal contracts produce a predictable, defensible line item that tenants can budget. Per-event billing produces mid-season surprise invoices that create tenant disputes.

Across three seasons of Ironwood commercial quotes, the seasonal contract has landed within 5–10% of a "typical" per-event winter — and 40–60% cheaper than a bad one.

## Strata Council Checklist for Ironwood Townhome Complexes

For the townhome stratas on Ironwood Drive, Wireless Way and Steveston Highway, sections 3 and 72 of the *Strata Property Act* place responsibility for common-property safety squarely on the corporation. That includes entrance courts, private lanes, mail-kiosk approaches and any shared walkway.

Before signing any Ironwood strata snow contract, verify:

- **WorkSafeBC clearance letter** — current, not expired.
- **$5,000,000 commercial general liability** with the strata named as additional insured.
- **Commercial auto liability** on every plow and salt unit.
- **Written trigger threshold** in centimetres, and a written response-time commitment from trigger.
- **De-icer product disclosure** — magnesium chloride, calcium chloride, calcium magnesium acetate, or rock salt? Get it in writing.
- **Subcontractor disclosure** — direct employees or subs? What are the subs' credentials?
- **Documentation deliverables** — GPS breadcrumbs, timestamped photos, weekly summaries, end-of-season AGM package.

If any one of those is missing, the contract is not defensible on a live claim.
