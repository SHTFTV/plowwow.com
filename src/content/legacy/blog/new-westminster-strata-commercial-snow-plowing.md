## Navigating the Legal Landscape: BC Occupiers Liability Act & Municipal Bylaws

In British Columbia, the *Occupiers Liability Act* dictates that property owners and managers have a "duty of care" to ensure that anyone entering the premises is reasonably safe. In the context of a New Westminster winter, this means having a defensible, documented snow and ice management plan. 

Furthermore, New Westminster City Bylaws require all owners or occupiers of businesses and multi-family residential buildings to remove snow and ice from sidewalks bordering their property no later than 10:00 AM following a snowfall. Failure to do so results in fines, but more importantly, it creates a "breach of statutory duty" that can be used against a strata in a civil lawsuit.

## Overcoming Common Operational Pitfalls

Two major issues plague New Westminster strata properties: **Underground parkade ramps** and **catch basin freeze-thaw cycles.**

1.  **Parkade Ramps:** In many New West high-rises, the entry ramp is exposed to the elements. These ramps become chutes of ice. If the heating cables (if present) fail, the ramp becomes a "no-go" zone for vehicles. [plowwow.com](https://plowwow.com) uses specialized calcium chloride pellets for these areas, as they generate heat upon contact and work at much lower temperatures than standard rock salt.
2.  **Catch Basins:** When snow melts during the day and freezes at night, it pools over clogged catch basins. This creates "ice ponds" in the middle of driving lanes. Our crews identify these basins during the pre-season and ensure they remain clear of snow piling.
