## Navigating the Legal Landscape: Liability and Bylaws

Under the **BC Occupiers Liability Act**, every property owner or manager in British Columbia must take "reasonable care" to keep residents, visitors, and delivery personnel safe. For a strata council overseeing sloped internal roadways, that standard is especially demanding — a single icy switchback can be the difference between a routine winter and a costly claim.

Walnut Grove falls under **Township of Langley** jurisdiction, where the **Township of Langley Traffic Bylaw** requires property owners and occupiers to clear snow and ice from adjacent sidewalks by **10:00 AM** the day following a snowfall. Non-compliance can bring fines and municipal clearing costs charged back to the property of **up to $1,000** per offence — a significant exposure for a strata corporation managing a large complex.

## Overcoming Common Operational Pitfalls

Managing a Walnut Grove property involves more than pushing snow from Point A to Point B. The bluff's slope and mature tree canopy create structural vulnerabilities unique to this part of Langley Township.

### Switchback & Slope Ice Formation
Many Walnut Grove strata developments were built with switchback interior roads to manage the bluff's grade. These tight, angled turns are the first spots to ice and the last to clear, and the #1 location for vehicle slides within the complex.

### Shaded Visitor Parking Refreeze
Visitor stalls tucked beneath mature tree canopy rarely see direct winter sun. When snow melts elsewhere in the complex during the day and refreezes overnight, these shaded stalls become isolated ice patches that a quick visual check can easily miss.
