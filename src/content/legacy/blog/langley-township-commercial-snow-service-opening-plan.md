# Township of Langley: Build Your Snow Plan Around Opening Time

*By the PlowWow editorial team · October 15, 2026.*

A cleared parking lane is only one part of opening a property after snowfall. Staff still need to reach the door, customers need a continuous walking route, and the person opening the building needs to know what remains unfinished. A commercial winter plan works best when those outcomes appear on the same site map.

## Start with the correct municipality

The Township of Langley says commercial and industrial sidewalks must be cleared by 10 a.m.; its FAQ includes institutional properties. This article concerns the Township, not Langley City. Check the current official guidance and the property classification before setting the service brief.

## Walk the opening sequence

Begin at the arrival point and follow the route to the entrance. Include accessible parking, curb ramps, loading doors and any steps between the parking area and reception. Mark a second entrance if the main route can be obstructed. Describe each segment with a name that the manager and operator will both recognize in a photograph or service update.

## Put hand work in the same plan as the truck

A truck may clear the vehicle lane while an entrance still needs a shovel or compact blower. List narrow paths, thresholds, stairs and gate openings separately. Give the provider the narrowest opening and any surface restrictions before equipment is assigned. The objective is a connected route, with a clear hand-off between machine work and detailed clearing.

## Resolve access before dispatch

Confirm gate arrangements, parked vehicles, delivery windows and who can authorize temporary restrictions. Share access codes privately with the assigned team. Mark curbs and fixed obstacles before snow conceals them. If a vehicle prevents completion, the report should name the blocked area and the person responsible for arranging the next step.

## Write down the response and return terms

Ask what starts service, which areas come first, how completion is reported and what prompts another assessment. PlowWow plans seasonal routes around a five-hour return target in normal conditions; the written agreement defines timing and scope. Keep that contractual target separate from the Township sidewalk requirement. A proposal should explain how the actual property schedule will be addressed.

## Make the handover useful to the opening team

A useful update identifies the visit time, completed areas, blocked access and outstanding actions. Agree who receives it and who checks a reported concern. Avoid telling occupants that every surface is clear merely because a truck attended. Reassess when site conditions change and use the property reporting process for new ice or access problems.

## Send a property brief, not just a postcode

For a PlowWow enquiry, include the Township address, operating hours, site plan, frontage, entrance photographs and any priority access needs. Identify the main decision-maker and a backup. Ask for written confirmation of available coverage and the included work before relying on a service arrangement.
