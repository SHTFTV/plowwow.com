## Why Yaletown Snow Removal Is Its Own Problem

Yaletown was built on the CPR rail-yard footprint in the 1890s. Every "cool" thing about it — the raised loading docks turned patios on Mainland and Hamilton, the cobble insets, the exposed brick, the narrow lanes — is a snow and ice liability magnet:

- **Loading-dock patios ice fast.** Raised platforms lose heat from all sides. A dusting on Mainland Street patios freezes to sheet ice within an hour of sunset.
- **Cobble insets can't be plowed.** Standard steel-edge plow blades destroy the character streetscape. Rubber-edge plows and walk-behind blowers are the only correct tools.
- **False Creek microclimate.** Yaletown gets the wet-snow / freezing-rain flip cycle more aggressively than any other downtown Vancouver neighborhood. That means salt runs matter more than plow runs.
- **Restaurant operators need dawn access.** Prep cooks arrive at 6 a.m., produce deliveries at 6:30, first covers seated by 11. There is no "we'll be there by noon."
