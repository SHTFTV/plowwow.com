# Zero-Degree Nights: Time to Flag the Curbs

![Original AI-generated PlowWow illustration of Wow beside marked curbs and a speed bump on bare pavement](/blog-images/zero-degree-nights-time-to-flag-the-curbs.jpg "PlowWow winter preparation: mark curbs and speed bumps")

**By the PlowWow team · November 21, 2026**

Local observations shared with PlowWow put temperatures at **0°C in parts of Burnaby, Port Coquitlam and Surrey two nights ago**, on November 19. Last night brought readings around **1°C**, and today still feels chilly.

For snow contractors and plow operators, this is the time to finish preparing sites—flagging the curbs, speed bumps and other obstacles while they’re still visible.

## What should be on the walkthrough checklist?

- Curbs, parking islands and entrance corners
- Speed bumps and concrete parking stops
- Drains, catch basins and raised covers
- Walkway edges and landscaped borders
- Hydrants and other fixed obstacles

Once snow covers a parking lot, those familiar features can become difficult to spot. Clear markers help operators recognize the boundaries and hazards beneath it. The [Snow & Ice Management Association’s staking guidance](https://resources.sima.org/hubfs/Member%20Downloads/Operations%20and%20Safety%20Downloads/Snow%20Business%20Tear%20and%20Teach%20Documents/Staking%20Best%20Practices.pdf?hsLang=en) identifies curbs, speed bumps, drainage features and other obstructions as areas to mark.

## Walk the property together

**Strata councils and property managers:** walk your property with your snow contractor. Point out changes since last winter, photograph existing damage and make sure the crew’s site notes match the markings on the ground.

A new speed bump or a moved parking stop is something the operator needs to know about before the blade reaches it. [BOSS’s snow-plowing guidance](https://info.bossplow.com/blog/eight-snow-plowing-safety-reminders) also recommends identifying and marking obstacles before snow hides them.
