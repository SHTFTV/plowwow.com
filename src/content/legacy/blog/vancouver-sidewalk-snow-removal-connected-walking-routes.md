## What must a Vancouver property clear?

Vancouver owners and occupants must remove snow and ice from the full width of adjoining sidewalks, including the side frontage of corner properties, by 10 a.m. each day when snow or freezing conditions apply. A narrow shovel track is not the City's stated standard. Check the [City's winter maintenance guidance](https://vancouver.ca/streets-transportation/snow-removal-from-city-streets.aspx) when assigning your frontage.

A road can be plowed while the last few metres to an entrance remain blocked. For a strata or commercial building, the useful winter question is whether people can travel continuously from the public sidewalk to the door, including those using mobility aids or pushing strollers.

## Why sidewalk connections deserve their own plan

CBC reporter Nipun Tiwari's October 6, 2026 report, “Saint John's revised plan for snowstorm cleanup puts more resources into clearing sidewalks,” describes additional attention to pedestrian access. The local lesson is practical: vehicle access and pedestrian access need separate resources. Saint John's response standards do not set Vancouver's rules.

Walk the route before winter. Start at the corner, follow the frontage, turn into the building approach and continue to the accessible entrance. Record gates, steps, ramps, drain outlets and places where a snow pile could interrupt the route. A parking-lot contract may not include those details unless they are written into its scope.

## Does a nearby park mean the City clears our frontage?

Proximity to a park does not settle responsibility. Separate the municipal park path, the adjoining public sidewalk and the private entrance path on a site plan. Confirm ownership and any maintenance agreements where the boundary is unclear.

Vancouver identifies priority pedestrian routes, including the Arbutus Greenway and parts of the seawall network. Its guidance also notes that some sections beside private property remain the adjoining owner's responsibility. A priority-route label therefore does not automatically remove a frontage obligation. Use the City's route information to check the actual segment rather than assuming the entire connection receives the same service.

For a hypothetical park-facing apartment building, the contractor might clear the building's approach and assigned frontage while a municipal crew maintains the park route. Record the handoff point. If the next section is blocked, report it to the responsible operator and communicate the available route to residents.

## Full-width clearance needs room for the snow

Survey where cleared snow can stay on the property without blocking the next entrance, a curb ramp or drainage. On a corner site, putting all the snow at the intersection can create a new obstruction. Include storage locations and a relocation trigger in the quote request.

Match equipment to the narrowest usable point, not the widest stretch. Stairs and tight entrance details need their own method. An ATV or UTV should only be considered where dimensions, surface capacity and authorized access permit it. See our [Vancouver equipment guide](/vancouver-strata-snow-equipment-sidewalks-parking-2026-2027) for the site measurements to collect.

## Put the entrance in the service schedule

Specify which surfaces need attention, when the building opens, who checks changing conditions and who authorizes return attendance. Agree how new accumulation or snow pushed back by road work will be handled. Keep dated service records and note sections that could not be reached.

The public-sidewalk deadline should not be presented as a universal rule for every private courtyard. Vancouver's municipal guidance distinguishes private-property snow clearing from its sidewalk bylaw. Plan private routes explicitly with the building manager rather than assuming the frontage schedule covers them.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is one shovel-width path enough in Vancouver?

The City calls for the full width of adjoining sidewalks to be cleared. Measure the frontage and identify obstacles before assigning equipment and crew time.

### Does living beside a park make the strata responsible for the whole park?

No automatic conclusion follows from adjacency. Confirm the public park boundary, frontage obligations and any agreements. Identify who maintains each connecting segment.

### Does a seasonal parking contract include pedestrian entrances?

Only if its written scope includes them. Ask for a route map and separate confirmation of stairs, ramps, frontage and return inspections.
