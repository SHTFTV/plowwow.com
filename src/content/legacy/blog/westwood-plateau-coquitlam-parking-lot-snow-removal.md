## The Plateau's Snow Reality

A few numbers worth knowing:

- The base of Westwood Plateau sits around 200 metres. The upper streets sit closer to 400 metres.
- For every 100 metres of elevation gain, temperatures drop roughly 0.6–1.0°C and snow accumulation increases by 25–50% in a typical Pacific frontal event.
- Westwood routinely sees 60–100 cm of total winter accumulation, against 15–30 cm at sea level in Vancouver.
- Refreeze cycles on the Plateau average **2–3 times** the count of refreeze cycles in downtown Vancouver across a winter.

In operational terms: a parking lot on Westwood Plateau needs more frequent service, more salting, and more aggressive snow relocation than the same-size lot in Coquitlam Centre or Burnaby. Contractors who don't adjust their pricing or operations for this routinely under-service Plateau accounts.

## Equipment Matched to Plateau Conditions

Plateau parking lots demand equipment that can handle:

- **Steep approach grades.** Many Plateau lots have driveways exceeding 10% grade. Plow trucks need chains, AWD, or 4WD; some sites require skid-steer-only service.
- **Deep wet snow.** Plateau snow is typically heavier and wetter than interior snow. Box-plow attachments and high-capacity blowers are standard.
- **Tight townhome lanes.** Many Plateau commercial-zoned lots back onto townhome complexes with shared lanes. Skid-steers with snow buckets replace full plow trucks where lane width is constrained.
- **Heavy stacking volume.** Plateau lots regularly need on-site stacking exceeding what flat-Lower-Mainland lots ever see. Stack-zone planning is part of every season-start walk-through.
