## Keep the cold flashback in its own year

Western Canada’s January 2024 Arctic outbreak belongs to the historical record, including [ECCC’s annual retrospective](https://www.canada.ca/en/environment-climate-change/services/ten-most-impactful-weather-stories/2024.html). It is not a forecast for Aldergrove this winter.

This October 29 revision keeps the article’s original publication date and historical URL, but changes its focus. Instead of another general service-scope guide, it examines a specific part of the Township’s published road response: the priority ladder can reverse when conditions worsen. That helps explain why the order seen on a street may differ from a simple expectation that every route gets one turn.

## A priority ladder is not a one-way queue

The [Township of Langley’s snow and ice page](https://www.tol.ca/en/services/snow-and-ice.aspx) places major collectors, arterials, school zones, bus routes and hilly areas first. Industrial and commercial roads appear in its second-priority description. It says resources return to higher priorities if conditions deteriorate.

For Township Aldergrove, that is a more informative starting point than assuming a visible truck’s next destination. The page describes a framework, not the location of a vehicle right now. It does not guarantee that a particular side street will be treated at a specific hour, and this article has not verified a live municipal fleet position.

![Original explanatory visual: Higher-priority routes come first; Worsening conditions can redirect; A fixed arrival does not follow](/blog-images/polar-vortex-aldergrove-january-2024-boundary-flashback-band-2.jpg)

## Why repeated work can be part of the published response

A one-way mental picture is easy: finish one road, move to the next, eventually finish everything. The Township’s wording allows a more changeable sequence. If conditions worsen, maintaining a higher-priority connection can take resources back from lower priorities.

That distinction matters when interpreting a future update. An observation that one route was treated twice is not, on its own, evidence of an error elsewhere. Equally, a policy is not proof that a particular route was treated successfully. A useful local report needs the location, time and source of the actual observation before making either claim.

## Aldergrove is a place name; an authority is more specific

Before applying a road policy, identify the actual jurisdiction and whether the surface is public or private. This article discusses the Township’s published framework. It does not assign that framework to a property under a different authority or decide an uncertain boundary.

That is also why a winter headline cannot settle the last part of a journey. A municipal road update and a privately managed entrance are different subjects. Neither a historic cold event nor a seasonal ocean signal proves the current condition of an entrance. Those facts have to come from a source that actually describes it.
