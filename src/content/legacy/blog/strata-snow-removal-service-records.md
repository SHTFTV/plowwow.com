# Strata Snow Removal: Keeping Useful Service Records

A strata’s winter file should help a council member or property manager understand what was agreed, what work was reported and what still needs attention. It does not need to be complicated. Consistent labels and a single place to save information are often more useful than a large folder of unconnected emails.

![PlowWow mascot beside a cleared strata walkway in a winter setting](/blog-images/strata-snow-removal-service-records.jpg "PlowWow winter property planning")

## Begin with the accepted scope

Keep the current agreement and dated site plan together. Identify included vehicle areas, pedestrian routes, service triggers, reporting arrangements and exclusions. Save the primary and backup operational contacts. If the scope changes, record the accepted change and its effective date so an older drawing is not mistaken for the current instructions.

## Agree on visit information in advance

Ask what the provider supplies after a visit. Useful details to discuss include the property, visit time, areas serviced, work performed and any access limitation. Confirm the format and where those records will be sent. If photographs or product information are part of the proposal, clarify when they are provided rather than assuming every visit includes the same evidence.

## Separate observations from assumptions

When residents report a concern, record what they observed, where it was and when. A photo and a precise location can help the property manager explain the issue. Distinguish that report from the contractor’s response and from any action that has actually been completed. Keeping those stages separate prevents a request from being mistaken for a resolved task.

## Track follow-up through to an answer

Use a short list with the date reported, location, assigned contact, response and current status. Note whether additional work falls within the existing scope or needs approval. When access was blocked, record what needs to change before another visit. Review recurring issues with the provider so the plan can be improved instead of recreating the same email chain during every event.

## Include the winter handover

At a management or council handover, share the current scope, contact list, unresolved items and the location of the service records. Keep private contact and access information in the appropriate restricted channel.
