## The Realities of the Campbell Heights Microclimate

Campbell Heights sits on the elevated plateau of South Surrey, closer to the US border than to the Fraser River, and that positioning changes its winter behavior compared to the river-corridor neighborhoods to the north. Being on higher, more exposed ground along the 192 Street corridor means the area catches **stronger, sustained arctic outflow winds** funneling up from the Nooksack Valley and across the open fields near the border, driving wind chill and snow drift across expansive industrial rooftops and parking fields.

## Navigating the Legal Landscape: Liability and Bylaws

Under the **BC Occupiers Liability Act**, every property owner or manager must take "reasonable care" to keep employees, visitors, and delivery drivers safe on the premises. For a large business park landlord, that duty extends across the entire leased footprint — parking fields, shared access roads, and loading zones alike.

Campbell Heights falls under Surrey jurisdiction, where the **City of Surrey Traffic Bylaw, 1997, No. 13007** requires property owners and occupiers to clear snow and ice from adjacent sidewalks and walkways by **10:00 AM** the day following a snowfall. Fines for non-compliance run **up to $500** per violation, and for a multi-tenant business park, an uncleared common walkway can expose the landlord to claims from every tenant on site.

## Overcoming Common Operational Pitfalls

Managing a Campbell Heights property involves more than pushing snow off pavement — the scale and exposure of the business park introduces failure points a smaller commercial site rarely faces.

### Wind-Driven Drift Re-Coverage
Because 192 Street sits on an exposed plateau, plowed snow on large lots frequently drifts back across cleared pavement within an hour of a plow visit, especially along building corners that funnel wind.

High volumes of truck traffic compact snow into a hard sheet on dock aprons faster than foot traffic ever could. Combined with rapid open-lot refreeze after sunset, an untreated dock apron becomes a slip and forklift hazard by early evening.
