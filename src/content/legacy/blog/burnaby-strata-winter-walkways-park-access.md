## When do Burnaby sidewalks need clearing?

Burnaby property owners must clear snow and ice from adjoining City sidewalks by 10 a.m. on the day following snowfall. The City's [uncleared-sidewalk reporting page](https://www.burnaby.ca/form/report-uncleared-sidewalks) states that deadline. For a strata, assign the frontage before winter and make sure the contractor's arrival plan addresses it.

Your internal pedestrian route needs separate attention. A clear parking aisle can still leave residents facing an untreated incline between a side entrance and the sidewalk. Build the service plan around how people actually leave and return to the property.

## The winter-news lesson for a Burnaby building

CBC's October 6, 2026 reporting by Nipun Tiwari describes Saint John directing more resources toward sidewalk clearing. It is a useful prompt for Burnaby councils: ask how much of your own winter plan is devoted to people walking. The news does not announce a change to Burnaby's bylaws.

Instead of copying a municipal road-plowing schedule into a private contract, identify your building's walking demands. Early departures, deliveries and evening access may require different arrangements. Establish who checks the route and how a resident can report a developing problem.

## Survey slopes and shaded approaches before snow arrives

Walk from each frequently used entrance to the public sidewalk. Mark changes in grade, stair landings, shaded corners and places where water crosses the paving. Photograph those points while drains and surface edges are visible.

Use a simple inspection sheet with the location, observed condition, time and action taken. Conditions can change after an initial visit, so the agreement should say who monitors and who may authorize more work. Product selection and application should reflect the surface, actual conditions and the manufacturer's directions; more material is not a substitute for a clear route.

Keep the pedestrian route distinct from vehicle work zones. When machinery is moving snow, residents need to understand where they can walk and which entrance remains available. A site-specific sequence is more useful than an unexplained promise to clear the entire development at once.

## What if our walkway leads toward Deer Lake or another park?

A park name is a useful location reference, not evidence that a particular path receives winter treatment. For a property near Deer Lake, check the specific connection on the ground: does it cross a public sidewalk, a municipal park trail or another parcel? Confirm the maintenance contact for each section.

Burnaby provides a separate reporting contact for hazardous conditions in parks and trails, park parking lots and sidewalks bordering City facilities. Use the [City's snow and ice page](https://www.burnaby.ca/services-and-payments/roads-and-traffic/snow-and-ice-control) to report the exact location. Do not promise residents that every recreational trail will be cleared on the same schedule as their frontage.

Where a park connection is unavailable, give residents accurate information about the available building exit and public route. Do not designate an alternate path across somebody else's property without authorization.

## Write a strata scope that can be inspected

Include a marked plan with numbered work areas: main entrance, accessible approach, side door, internal walkway and public frontage. For each, record the agreed work, access limits, storage location and inspection responsibility. Identify steps or narrow passages that require hand work.

Confirm that snow relocation will not obstruct drainage or bury the route that has just been opened. If visitor stalls form part of the planned snow-storage area, resolve that conflict before awarding the contract. Read the [Burnaby equipment and parkade guide](/burnaby-commercial-strata-snow-equipment-2026-2027) when deciding how crews can reach the work.

## Match booking arrangements to the route

A seasonal agreement can define ongoing monitoring and response responsibilities. A five-visit option needs a clear definition of a visit, and a single attendance needs an agreed scope and availability check. None should be assumed to include unlimited return visits.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Does the 10 a.m. deadline cover every internal strata path?

The cited municipal guidance concerns adjoining City sidewalks. Define private walkways separately in the property maintenance plan and service agreement.

### Will the City clear the park trail beside our building?

Check the particular trail with the City. A nearby park does not establish a guaranteed treatment schedule for every connecting path.

### What information helps a contractor quote a sloped walkway?

Provide photographs, dimensions, surface type, access restrictions, drainage details and the hours the route is needed. Include the inspection and return-service arrangements you expect.
