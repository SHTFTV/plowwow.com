## 2. The Realities of the White Rock Microclimate

White Rock’s geography creates a specialized microclimate. Unlike the relatively flat expanses of Richmond or Delta, White Rock is defined by its steep elevation gain from the Marine Drive waterfront up to the North Bluff plateau. This incline, combined with constant marine moisture, creates a "flash freeze" phenomenon that is unique to the peninsula.

As moist air blows off the Pacific, it hits the cold landmass. In many cases, the temperature might be +1°C at the beach but -2°C at the top of the hill. This leads to heavy, wet snow that compacts into ice under the weight of vehicles, followed by a rapid freeze-thaw cycle as the sun reflects off the water.

## 3. Navigating the Legal Landscape: Occupiers Liability & Bylaws

For a White Rock strata council, "ignorance of the law" is an expensive defense. The BC Occupiers Liability Act mandates that property owners take "reasonable care" to ensure people are safe on their premises. White Rock municipal bylaws are also stringent. Property owners are generally required to clear snow and ice from sidewalks adjacent to their land by 10:00 AM following a snow event. ## 6. Overcoming Common Operational Pitfalls

In White Rock, two architectural features cause the most winter headaches: **Underground Parkade Ramps** and **Catch Basins**.

**The Parkade Ramp Trap:**
Many White Rock stratas have steep ramps leading to underground parking. Because these are often shielded from the sun but exposed to the wind, they become "ice chutes." If the drainage at the bottom of the ramp is frozen, the meltwater from the street above pools at the garage door and freezes, trapping residents inside or out.

**The Catch Basin Failure:**
When snow melts during a White Rock "thaw" day, the water carries salt and debris toward the catch basins. If the temperature drops at sunset (constant in the microclimate), the basin freezes solid, creating a localized flood event during the next rainfall.
