## Why North Vancouver needs specialized snow & ice management

North Vancouver requires specialized snow and ice management because its dramatic elevation changes and coastal proximity create localized weather patterns where it can be raining at the SeaBus terminal but snowing heavily in Lynn Valley. Unlike flatter regions of Metro Vancouver, the North Shore's steep topography makes traditional plowing difficult and standard salting techniques insufficient against "black ice" on hills.

The North Shore mountains act as a barrier, trapping cold air and creating significant accumulations that other parts of the Lower Mainland rarely see. During the record snowfalls of December 2022 and January 2024, North Vancouver saw several centimeters of accumulation in hours, paralyzing transit along Highway 1 and blocking crucial commuter routes. Without a dedicated professional service, North Vancouver property owners face significant liability risks and potential fines from the City or District.

Furthermore, the "wet" nature of North Shore snow—often referred to as "West Coast cement"—is incredibly heavy. Shovelling this by hand is not only exhausting but can be dangerous for those not accustomed to the physical strain. Professional equipment is necessary to move this heavy slush before it freezes solid overnight, creating an impenetrable layer of ice that can last for weeks in shaded areas like the North Lonsdale canyons.

## Local bylaws and liability — what North Vancouver property owners must know

In North Vancouver, snow removal is not just a courtesy; it is a legal requirement. Both the City and District of North Vancouver have specific bylaws governing the removal of snow and ice from sidewalks adjacent to your property.

*   **The City of North Vancouver (CNV):** Bylaw 5283 requires owners or occupiers of any premises to remove snow and ice from sidewalks surrounding their property within 24 hours of a snowfall. For commercial properties, this often must be done even sooner to ensure public safety.
*   **The District of North Vancouver (DNV):** Similar to the City, the District mandates that residents and businesses clear sidewalks within 24 hours. Failure to do so can result in fines and the costs of the District clearing the snow themselves.
*   **Liability:** Under the *Occupiers Liability Act* of British Columbia, property owners have a "duty of care" to ensure that anyone entering their property is reasonably safe. A build-up of ice on a steep North Vancouver driveway or walkway is a significant liability risk. Using a professional service like PlowWow provides a professional record of mitigation, which is your best defense against potential claims.
