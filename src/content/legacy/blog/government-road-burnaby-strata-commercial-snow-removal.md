## Government Road's Snow Profile — Why This Corner of Burnaby Is Different

Government Road isn't a standard Burnaby residential grid. Its topography, canopy and land use produce a distinct set of winter risks:

- **Old-growth canopy shade.** Douglas fir, western red cedar and hemlock line every block. North-facing sidewalks and driveways don't see direct sun for months.
- **Burnaby Lake cold pool.** The lake basin traps colder air. Overnight lows on Government Road run 3–4 °C below Metrotown at the same elevation.
- **Large-lot driveways.** Estate driveways here are often 40–60 metres long and slope toward the road. A single freeze-thaw turns them into luge runs.
- **Hillside strata approach roads.** The townhome complexes tucked between Government and Winston sit above lake grade and share long, private approach roads that municipal plows never touch.
- **Business park loading yards.** The Burnaby Lake business park mixes distribution, light-industrial and office tenants who need trucks moving before 7 a.m. Frozen loading yards cost a shift, not a morning.
- **Deer and wildlife crossings.** Rock salt on Government Road walkways draws deer and puts pets at risk. Product choice matters here more than almost anywhere else in Burnaby.

## Estate & Business Park Liability — Occupiers Liability Act and Burnaby Bylaw Duty

Government Road property owners carry two overlapping duties every Burnaby winter.

Under the *Occupiers Liability Act* (BC), an occupier owes a duty to take that care that in all the circumstances of the case is reasonable to see that a person on the premises will be reasonably safe. In estate and business-park case law, "reasonable" has meant a documented pre-open clearing protocol on any surface where invitees, contractors or the public are expected.

Under City of Burnaby Bylaw No. 6577 (Traffic Bylaw) and successor consolidations, property owners are responsible for clearing snow and ice from public sidewalks adjacent to their property within a defined window after a snowfall. Estate frontages on Government Road and industrial frontages in the business park are both covered.

What this means for a Government Road landowner or landlord:

- **Business park tenants open before 7 a.m.** A contract that promises "morning" service without a written pre-open time is not defensible for a distribution tenant.
- **Estate liability travels with invitees.** Contractors, nannies, delivery drivers and family are all covered under the Act — a cleared driveway is table stakes.
- **Photo documentation isn't optional.** Both slip-and-fall and vehicle-damage claims subpoena the service log.

## The Seasonal Contract Case for Government Road

For a Government Road estate or business park landlord, the per-event vs. seasonal decision comes down to three numbers:

1. **Pre-open guarantee.** Seasonal customers are cleared first. Per-event customers are cleared *after* seasonal — often at 9 or 10 a.m. instead of 6 a.m. In the business park, that gap costs a shift.
2. **Freezing-rain dispatches.** Government Road's canopy holds moisture. Refreeze mornings are more common than fresh snow. Seasonal contracts dispatch on those forecasts. Per-event contracts don't.
3. **Predictable budget line.** For a business park landlord, snow feeds directly into CAM recovery. Seasonal contracts produce a predictable, defensible number. For estate owners, a fixed seasonal price ends the "how much is this storm going to cost me" conversation before it starts.
