## What is Surrey's sidewalk-clearing requirement?

Surrey directs residents and businesses to clear sidewalks around their properties before 10 a.m. after snowfall. Its [winter operations guidance](https://www.surrey.ca/services-payments/parking-streets-transportation/winter-snow-ice-control-operations) also explains that road plows can push snow back onto a previously cleared driveway or sidewalk. Your property plan should account for that possible second obstruction.

For a commercial site, completing the parking lot is only part of the job. Staff, customers and visitors still need a usable path from the frontage or parking area to the entrance. Agree who checks that connection after the main clearing pass.

## A sidewalk lesson from the Saint John news

Nipun Tiwari's October 6, 2026 CBC report on Saint John's revised winter plan highlights additional resources for pedestrian routes. The useful Surrey application is continuity: a well-cleared road cannot compensate for a blocked building approach. Surrey's own requirements remain the source for local deadlines.

Consider a retail or industrial property with several driveways. A crew may clear the front walk early, then passing road equipment may leave a ridge at a crossing. If the contract only records one completed pass, responsibility for returning can be unclear. Resolve that question before the first event.

## Plan for snowbanks at driveway crossings

List every place people cross a vehicle entrance. Include the path from accessible parking, visitor parking and the public sidewalk. Mark the sections where pushed snow could accumulate, and decide how crews will work without directing pedestrians into active vehicle movements.

A practical sequence is to identify the temporary pedestrian route, clear the agreed vehicle areas, restore the crossing points and inspect the entrance connection. The contractor should adapt that sequence to the site's traffic and equipment. Keep a contact available to authorize work if conditions differ from the plan.

Surrey advises placing shoveled snow on the property rather than the street. Designate storage areas on your plan, accounting for doors, sightlines and pedestrian access. Where space is insufficient, obtain a separate relocation or hauling scope instead of assuming each pass can push snow farther away.

## Check where meltwater goes

The City warns that blocked catch basins can allow water to build up, freeze or flood nearby properties. Mark drainage points before they are obscured by snow, and include a check after piles begin to melt. A route that looked clear earlier may need another inspection when conditions change.

Ask who records those inspections and how problems are escalated. Use actual site conditions and the agreed treatment plan when deciding whether salt or a suitable brine application is needed. Clearing, treatment and monitoring are separate tasks that should be recognizable in a quote.

## Where does a park or public walking route fit?

A building beside a park may have visitors arriving through a route the property manager does not control. Map the boundary between the private entrance, the assigned sidewalk frontage and the municipal path. Confirm responsibility for any shared connection or easement rather than treating every nearby path as part of the private contract.

Report a blocked public section with an exact location through Surrey's winter reporting channels. For a private connecting walkway, identify the responsible manager and agree the handoff. Do not claim that a nearby road's priority means the park path will receive the same treatment.

## Make return attendance part of the booking discussion

Ask these questions when comparing commercial proposals:

- Are sidewalks and driveway crossing points explicitly included?
- Who checks for snow pushed back after municipal plowing?
- What triggers another visit, and who can authorize it?
- Are materials, relocation and pedestrian hand work priced separately?
- What completion records identify the areas serviced and any access limitations?

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Can a road plow block an entrance we already cleared?

Yes. Surrey's winter guidance explicitly describes snow being pushed back onto driveways or sidewalks. Include an inspection and return-work arrangement in the property plan.

### Does one booked visit mean the route stays clear all day?

No. Further snowfall, road clearing and changing surface conditions can require another attendance. Confirm visit limits and monitoring responsibilities in the quote.

### Should we push snow onto the street to free parking stalls?

Surrey directs people to keep cleared snow on their property rather than the street. Agree storage or relocation arrangements before work starts.
