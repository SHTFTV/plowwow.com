# Fairview Slopes Strata Snow Removal: Hills and Access

![PlowWow mascot in a winter illustration of a sloping Vancouver residential street](/blog-images/fairview-slopes-strata-snow-removal-hills-access.jpg "Fairview Slopes winter access planning with PlowWow")

## Fairview Slopes snow removal starts with access

A steep approach, a narrow lane and a parkade entrance can turn a small strata property into a demanding winter stop. For Fairview Slopes buildings, the useful question is not simply who will clear the snow. It is how the crew will reach the agreed work areas when hills are icy, parked vehicles reduce space or a permitted approach changes. PlowWow reviews those details when preparing a property-specific winter-service proposal.

## Map the route to your actual entrance

Give the building address and identify each entrance, parkade approach, rear gate and pedestrian route separately. For properties around West 6th, West 7th and West 8th avenues, describe the relevant cross street and which side of the building provides access. A frontage address does not necessarily identify the driveway or service entrance. Keep entry codes and security instructions out of public comments; provide them directly to the service contact.

## Plan for hills before the first dispatch

Mark steep driveway transitions, steps, shaded sections and places where water collects or crosses a walkway. Ask for a site assessment of the surfaces and the equipment needed to reach them. Truck access alone does not establish how a stairway or narrow internal path will be serviced. The accepted plan should identify pedestrian work as well as vehicle areas, and state what happens if an approach is unsafe.

## Street closures need a live check

Roadworks, events and construction can change the permitted approach to a property. Check the City of Vancouver’s current road-closure information and the Broadway Subway construction notices when planning access, then flag relevant changes to your contractor. An old site map is not permission to enter a closed street. Alternate access must be permitted and suitable for the vehicle, equipment and conditions; never assume a crew can drive through a closure or use a pedestrian connection.

## Give blocked access a clear next step

Identify one council or property-management contact and a backup. Agree how the crew reports blocked or unsafe access, who can arrange removal of an obstruction and how a return visit is authorized. If the main approach becomes unavailable, the contractor should assess the agreed alternative rather than improvise a risky descent or a turn without enough space. Discuss how any resulting change in timing or scope will be communicated.

## Keep people moving between the street and the lobby

Label the public frontage, entrance steps, handrails, shared paths and accessible entrance where present. Identify safe snow-storage areas during the assessment so cleared snow does not obstruct an entrance, sightline or another agreed route. Note drains and meltwater paths for review. Ask how repeat ice concerns will be reported and whether additional checks or treatments are included in the quote.

## Separate sidewalk duties from the post-storm window

Vancouver’s current winter guidance calls for full-width clearing of adjacent public sidewalks by 10 a.m. daily when snow or freezing conditions affect them, including side frontage on corner lots. Confirm who is responsible for that frontage. PlowWow plans around a five-hour service standard after the end of a storm event, with the event-end trigger, covered tasks and access requirements defined in the agreement. That post-storm window is separate from the arrangements needed for public sidewalks and priority work while a storm continues.

## Book a Fairview Slopes site review

Send the address, marked photos or a site plan, driveway and gate details, known access restrictions and the areas you want included. Tell PlowWow about decorative or new concrete so the surface can be considered when discussing treatment. Ask the proposal to cover triggers, service areas, repeat visits, snow storage, relocation, records and blocked-access procedures. Availability and the accepted schedule are confirmed for each property; an enquiry does not reserve a route position.

## Quick answers for strata councils

Can a crew always reach a steep building? Access depends on the conditions and the assessed approach; no blanket promise replaces a site plan. Will construction detours be the same all winter? Check current official notices and communicate changes. Does a seasonal contract include unlimited returns? Only if the accepted proposal explicitly includes them.
