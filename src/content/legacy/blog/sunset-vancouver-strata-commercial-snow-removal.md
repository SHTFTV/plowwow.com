## Why Sunset Snow Removal Is Different

Sunset is not a generic Vancouver neighbourhood — its microclimate, street grid and property mix shape how snow and ice have to be handled:

- **Cold-sink microclimate.** Sunset runs from Main Street east to Knight Street and from 41st Avenue south to Marine Drive — a long, low, flat basin that sits between the Cambie ridge to the north and the Fraser River escarpment to the south. Cold air pools here overnight and refreeze events routinely start earlier and hold longer than at higher-elevation neighbourhoods like Riley Park.
- **Corridor commercial density.** Fraser Street from 41st to Marine and the Main Street south of 41st carry a wall of independent retail, restaurants and mid-block strip plazas. Each frontage carries its own sidewalk-clearing obligation under City of Vancouver bylaw.
- **Post-war family stratas and laneway homes.** Sunset has one of Vancouver's fastest-growing laneway-house and multiplex portfolios sitting alongside a heavy stock of 30- to 60-unit low-rise stratas from the Fraser Street corridor west. Both surface types show up on the same block.
- **Bylaw exposure.** City of Vancouver Street and Traffic By-law No. 2849, section 76, requires the owner or occupier of property to clear adjacent public sidewalks by 10:00 a.m. the day after a snowfall. Sunset's long block faces make missed sidewalks especially visible and especially litigious.
- **Pet-heavy park blocks.** Memorial South Park, Sunset Park and the John Oliver Secondary walking routes get walked in every weather. Rock salt burns paws and stains lobby tile — PlowWow uses pet-safe magnesium chloride and calcium magnesium acetate as standard.
- **Freezing-rain refreeze.** Fraser River fog rolling up the escarpment turns marginal events into freezing-rain events far more often than five kilometres north on the Cambie corridor.

## BC Strata Property Act Liability — What Sunset Boards Need to Know

Under sections 3 and 72 of the *Strata Property Act*, a strata corporation is responsible for the repair and maintenance of common property. In case law across BC, that responsibility has been repeatedly interpreted to include a duty to keep sidewalks, walkways, parkade ramps and entrance courts reasonably free of snow and ice during winter events.

What this means in practice for a Sunset strata council:

- **Documented service is the defense.** When a slip-and-fall claim is filed, the strata's insurer will ask for the service log — dispatch times, GPS breadcrumbs, salt-application photos, product used, technician on site. Without a written seasonal contract and per-visit documentation, the insurer's defense position collapses.
- **Per-event contractors create gaps.** A council that pays "as needed" is implicitly deciding when it thinks service is needed. That decision, made by a volunteer board on a Sunday night, is exactly the exposure plaintiffs' counsel targets.
- **Seasonal contracts move the decision.** With a PlowWow seasonal contract, the trigger threshold is defined in writing (typically 2 cm of accumulation or a forecast freezing-rain event), and dispatch is automatic. The council is no longer the decision-maker on a live event — the contract is.
- **Council minutes matter.** Every Sunset strata board should record, in AGM minutes, that a seasonal snow contract with defined response times has been reviewed and approved. That paper trail is a meaningful part of any future defense.

## Credentials to Verify Before Signing Any Sunset Contract

Before a strata council or commercial landlord in Sunset signs a snow contract with anyone, verify:

- **WorkSafeBC (WCB) clearance letter.** No clearance letter, no contract — full stop. Under the *Workers Compensation Act*, the property owner can be assessed for the contractor's unpaid WCB premiums if a worker is injured on site.
- **Commercial general liability insurance.** Minimum $5,000,000, with the strata corporation or landlord named as additional insured. Ask for the certificate.
- **Automobile liability.** Every plow truck and salt unit needs commercial auto coverage.
- **Written service specification.** Trigger threshold (cm), response time from trigger, surfaces included, de-icer product, documentation delivered.
- **De-icer product disclosure.** Rock salt (NaCl), calcium chloride, magnesium chloride, calcium magnesium acetate — each has different pet, plant and concrete impacts. Get the answer in writing.
- **Subcontractor policy.** Some low-price bidders route your site to whichever subcontractor is closest that morning. Ask whether the crew on your site will be direct employees or subs — and whether subs carry the same WCB and CGL coverage.
